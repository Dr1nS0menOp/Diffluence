# Wave PB1: draft and published spaces, the pair and one page

Builder report for spec 073, wave PB1. It covers what was built, what was measured, what was decided on the product owner's behalf, and what is not proven yet.

## What was built

**The pair and the managed space.** Migration 0061 adds `space_pairs`, `page_pairs`, `file_pairs`, `release_runs` and `release_run_pages`, owned by the new module `internal/publishing`, and a nullable `spaces.pair_role`. A space administrator of both spaces links a draft space to a published space: a new one (made in the same transaction, visible to its maker alone) or an existing space that holds nothing but a home page with one version. A second pair on either side, a chain, a self-pair, two drafts into one, a personal space and a space with content are refused with a code and a sentence that says what is in the way. Linking pairs the two home pages, releases nothing, and withdraws the draft space's public sites and links.

The published space is managed three times over:

- `permissions.Evaluate` refuses every write of content in a space whose side is `published`, for everyone, instance administrators included; a person who could otherwise write gets 409 `managed_space`. A draft space never gets `site.publish`.
- `perm_editable_pages` leaves out published spaces; the permission matrix gains the space kinds draft and published against every action, in Go and in SQL.
- A trigger on `pages`, `page_versions`, `attachments` and `attachment_versions` refuses inserts and content changes in a published space unless the transaction set `diffluence.release` locally (SQLSTATE `DF073`, mapped to 409 `managed_space` by the API). Columns that are not content (owner, review dates, closing comments) stay free, so the daily upkeep keeps running.

**`space.release`**, split off `space.administer` with the migration proof of waves PM, FU and DC1 (`rbac_split_off`, `rbac_words`, `rbac_split_roles_from`, `SplitFrom`, `WithSplitOff`, bundles version 7). `TestSplittingOffReleaseChangesNoDecision` snapshots every decision before and after: nothing changes except the new permission where administer was.

**Out of step.** `page_pairs` keeps the facts of the last release (version, content hash of the stored document, title, parent, look, labels). A trigger on `pages` and `page_labels` (`page_pair_refresh`) recomputes the aspects that differ in the statement of each change, so undoing a change makes the page released again. Comments, statuses, unsaved drafts and restrictions never count.

**Releasing one page.** `POST /pages/{id}/release/preview` keeps a preview for an hour with the version it shows; `POST /pages/{id}/release` releases exactly that version, in one transaction, under a lock on the pair row. It makes the twin under the twin of the nearest released ancestor, copies the files the document shows when they changed (a new version of the earlier copy, so its id stays), rewrites the document (links and cards to twins or to their words, blocks that name pages or the space, addresses into the draft space, instruction text and placeholders removed, files to their copies), passes it through `document.Normalize`, and writes one version with the releaser as author and the note as summary. Only place, look or labels changed: no version. In step: nothing.

**Links.** The attributes that hold page references are one list in `internal/document/references.go`, and the dynamic-block settings of a page are read from the block schema itself. A guard test over every block cleaner fails when an attribute keeps an id that no list names. A fuzz test over random documents with every kind of reference proves that rewriting and the gate always succeed and that no draft id or key survives.

**Audit.** The draft space records `space.paired`, `release.previewed`, `release.page` (both sides' ids, versions, content hashes, aspects, note) and `space.unpaired`; the published space records `space.managed`, `page.released` and `attachment.released` with the twin's ids only. No entry holds page text.

**The switch.** The experimental part `publishing`, off on new and upgraded installations. Every route answers 404 `experimental_off` while it is off, except ending a pair (`POST /spaces/{id}/publishing/end`), which stays open; turning the part off keeps published spaces read-only.

**Screens.** Space settings, Content, Published space (link with preview, settings of the pair, who may release, health, end the link); the release band under the byline of a draft page; "Release this page…" in the More menu with its preview dialog; the "Released" line on a twin with "Open the draft" for those who may see it; the checkbox in Experimental parts. Every word is in `en.json`. The space key setting now says "Free the old key" (D12).

## Tests

All written against the behaviour of the spec; the failure paths and leak paths first. The 29 mutations of this wave (in `scripts/mutation-check.sh` under "Spec 073") each break one rule and name the test that must catch it.

- `internal/app/publishing_test.go`: 25 tests, among them the leak test that renders 21 doors of the published space for one of its readers and searches each answer for the draft space's id, key and name, the draft page ids and words that exist only in the draft; the trigger refusing eight writes an older node would make; refusals of the managed rule for an instance administrator, a space administrator and an editor; out of step aspect by aspect with each undo; files; the previewed version; two runs at once; a failed run leaving nothing; the query counts.
- `internal/permissions`: the matrix with draft and published spaces, the adoption test of the split.
- `internal/publishing`: the rewrite, the placement, no cycle over 200 random trees, and `FuzzRewriteLeavesNoDraftID`.
- `internal/document`: the guard over the block schema.
- `web/e2e/publishing.spec.ts` and four pictures in `web/e2e/0-design.spec.ts`.

## Upgrade impact

Migration 0061 is expand only. It adds the tables, the column, the triggers and `space.release` for every holder of `space.administer`; nothing changes for anyone until an administrator turns the part on. A node of the previous release beside the new one cannot write into a published space either: the trigger refuses it and that node shows its ordinary error. Migration 0060 holds the number of wave GT1.

## Decided on the product owner's behalf on 2026-11-14

D13 to D22 in the decision table of spec 073, each "decided on the product owner's behalf on 2026-11-14, may be overruled". In short: facts of out of step kept by a trigger; place is the parent only until PB2; one page released in the request; `managed_space` names no space; what the managed rule refuses; who counts for the warning that drafts are readable; a preview belongs to its maker; archived and never-approved pages refused until PB2 and PB3; how files of other spaces and gone files are released; the three open questions of the spec left with the product owner.

## Not proven, and not built

- **Real data.** None of the budgets were measured on the real-data dump with its largest space paired (PUB-1427, PUB-1108); the dump was not available. The numbers above are on generated data.
- **The previous binary (PUB-1306, PUB-1307).** The trigger is proven with the statements an older node would send, written out in SQL, not with the binary of the previous release; the N/N+1 upgrade test was not extended, and a rollback was not run.
- **A run read from another connection while it runs (PUB-450).** Proven indirectly: a run that fails after making its twin and copying a file leaves nothing (`TestAFailedReleaseLeavesNothing`).
- **Place among siblings** (PUB-302 position, PUB-412) is not compared in PB1 (D14).
- **Not built in PB1:** the note for would-be editors on a twin (PUB-1231), "released from draft version N" in the twin's history for those who see the draft and the mark of released versions in the draft's history (PUB-332 in part, PUB-1223), the warning about links into other draft spaces and about headings not released yet (PUB-520, PUB-531), import into a published space as its own test (PUB-1041, refused by the same rule), guests as writers and readers as their own matrix cases (PUB-612), copying a twin (PUB-631), and the end of a pair with its preview (PUB-243 is PB2; the end itself is built because PB1 needs it open while the part is off).
- **Load of the triggers on bulk writes** (an import of thousands of pages) was not measured.
