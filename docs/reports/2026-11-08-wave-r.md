# Wave R: roles you make yourself

Spec 036. Roles for a space and for the instance, the migration of every grant, four permissions split out of space administration, every permission in plain words, and the screens in the settings pattern of spec 033. The proof that nobody gains or loses access comes first.

## What was found in the study

- Single grants lived in `space_grants` and `global_grants`, written in four packages: `permissions`, `exchange` (bundle import), and `identity` (new accounts, service accounts, global permissions, deleting a group). Nothing remembered where a grant came from.
- `permissions.Templates` and the own sets of `permission_sets` only filled in boxes. Changing a set changed nobody.
- The rule is held to one rule by `matrix_test.go`: Evaluate, the `perm_*` SQL and a literal transcription. Changing what Evaluate reads would have meant changing all three, so the study chose to keep the grant tables as what roles add up to, kept equal by the database.
- `space.administer` guards some twenty-five things, from the name of a space to its public sites, automation, templates and audit. Spec 036 lists what each broad permission guards, and which parts a space may want to give apart.
- Raw keys reached screens in three places: who holds which global permission, the box for named sets, and the dialog for changing a person's type.

## What was built

- Migration `0036_roles.sql`: `roles`, `role_assignments`, triggers that keep grants equal to roles (`rbac_sync`), a scope check on assignments, `rbac_set_exact` and `rbac_add_permissions` for the older code, `rbac_adopt` for the migration, `rbac_drift` to list any difference, `rbac_words` to name a role in the words of the interface, `rbac_uuid7` for ids, and the four built-in space roles.
- `db.GrantApp` revokes writing grants from the application role. The triggers run as the owner.
- `internal/permissions/roles.go`: making, changing, previewing and deleting roles; giving roles in a space and for the instance; who holds a role and where; every change audited with before and after.
- Four permissions split out of `space.administer`: `site.publish`, `space.automation`, `space.templates`, `space.audit`. Each replaces administering in its module checks and route rules, works where administering worked, is held by the built-in Space admin, and was given by the migration to everyone who administered. `permissions.SplitFrom` names the pairs. Ten more are proposed in spec 036 and blueprint 05 (PRM-206 to PRM-215), not built.
- Bundles are format 2; a bundle of format 1 gives the split-off permissions with administering.
- Plain words: a name and one line for every permission in `en.json`, six groups in `web/src/permission-words.ts`.
- Screens: Administration > Roles (the former Permissions tab) with the role tables, an editor with grouped checkboxes and who holds the role, who holds which instance role, and who holds which instance permission in words. A space's settings give roles under People and roles, and show each part to whoever holds what it needs.
- Ten new routes for roles, assignments and holders, and new rules on the routes of sites, public links, rules, webhooks, space templates and the space audit, in `docs/api/routes.txt` and `openapi.json`.

## Measured and proven

- **No decision changed in the migration:** 369,861 decisions compared, 24,951 allowed, 720 of those through a split-off permission (`TestAdoptingGrantsChangesNoDecision`). The grants were written as before roles, without the split-off permissions, in every shape beside restrictions, drafts, groups, all members, all guests, global grants in four shapes and two own sets. After adoption every decision is what it was, and each split-off permission decides exactly as administering did.
- **One rule:** the matrix test compares 900,315 cases of Evaluate with the literal rule and the SQL, with the four new permissions among the actions and a grant case that holds them without administering. The query budget test of ARC-239 passes without a change to its budgets; reading a space now costs fewer queries than before.
- **The split narrows only:** someone who administers a space without the four is refused all seven routes they guard; someone who holds one of them reaches its routes and is refused the others (`internal/app/finer_permissions_test.go`).
- **Words:** every permission has a name and a line (Go test), the database names roles in the same words (Go test), and the groups hold every named permission once (unit test).
- **The application role cannot write a grant**, and can still give a role and delete a space (`TestApplicationRoleGrantsOnlyThroughRoles`).
- **Twenty-six mutations** cover the rules of roles, the migration, the triggers, the split, bundles and who may read the holders of a role.

## Found on the way

- The new space route answered a stranger with 400 before checking access, which told them the space exists. The access check now comes first; `TestEveryObjectRouteIsProtected` found it.
- Anonymising a person left their id in `role_assignments`. It stays there on purpose, for the same reason as in `space_grants`: taking it away could change who may do what. The list of decided columns says so.
- Making a space template checks the name before the permission, so someone without the permission learns the name was wrong before being refused. It tells them nothing about the space they can see; left as it was.
- Reading a space asked the database for the person and the space once per capability. It now loads them once.

## Not proven

- A role held by thousands of subjects, changed at once: its cost grows with the holders and was not measured at that size.
- Two administrators changing the same role at the same moment: the row lock decides, and no test runs them in parallel.
- Whether automation, publishing and templates should keep working in an archived space: they do, as administering did, because the split had to change nothing.
