## What was built

- **The model (DOC-700, DOC-721).** A transition names `signature_meaning`. On an author's step its taker signs; on the step that follows an approved review, every approving reviewer signs. The validator refuses a meaning that is not a key and a signed request for changes. Four meanings every space has; a space adds its own (`signature_meanings`), never changed or removed, and a workflow names only meanings its space has. "Two-step review" signs its quality approval.
- **Who may sign (DOC-014, DOC-751, DOC-760, DOC-1422).** `signing_rights`: an administrator enables a person and chooses how they identified them; never their own, never a guest or service account. A signature also needs the right to take its step.
- **The confirmation (DOC-730 to DOC-733).** `signing_confirmations` in identity. `POST /pages/{id}/signing` begins one signature, bound to the page, the version and the step or the stage of the round, in the person's own session. It is confirmed with `POST /signing/confirm` (password and authenticator code), `POST /signing/passkey` (user verification required), or `GET /auth/oidc/sign` (a login at the identity provider after the step began, for someone without a password). The step that signs takes it once, inside its own transaction, within two minutes. Wrong passwords and codes count toward the stop after 100. A token, a rule, the system or a session that still has to set up a second factor is refused at the signature itself, not only at the route.
- **The record (DOC-710 to DOC-712, DOC-1225).** `signatures` holds the signer, the printed name of the moment, the meaning and its words, the time, the page, version, workflow version, step, round and stage, the method, the content hash and its form, and its audit entry, which holds the hash too. The record and the step are one transaction. Records cannot be changed or deleted (a trigger), and neither can the hash a version keeps (`version_hashes`).
- **The hash (DOC-711, DOC-1303).** Canonical form 1 over the title, the document format version, the document as canonical JSON and the files the version pins with their version, name and SHA-256, each part with its length. It is computed at the first signature of a version and kept.
- **Showing and verifying (DOC-740, DOC-741, DOC-1010).** The band shows the signatures of the version on display with a mark; the history lists them per version, with Verify signatures; every export's control line names them. A full verification recomputes the hash and checks the audit entry: what it says, its own hash and its neighbours. The control history, for `space.audit`, lists every audit entry of the page with its place in the chain. `diffluence signatures verify` checks every signature.
- **The auditor's export (DOC-742, DOC-1020, DOC-1021).** `GET /spaces/{id}/auditor-export` for a space, page or branch and a period, as JSON Lines; audited with its scope and period; at most 1000 pages. `scripts/verify-signatures.mjs` and the procedure in `docs/operations/regulated-use.md` verify it outside Diffluence.
- **Keeping (DOC-770, DOC-771).** A signed version cannot be removed; a file a signed version shows cannot be deleted or moved; a trash item that holds signed records is never purged (refused by hand, skipped when its time ends); `diffluence audit prune` keeps the log from the first signature on; an erased signer's records keep the name they signed under, and the dialog says so.
- **Screens (DOC-720, DOC-923).** The signing dialog names the meaning, version and title in its heading and button, links to the exact text, asks for the confirmation in the same dialog, and says a failure in a live region; the band, which is a status region, shows the signature after. The workflow editor has a choice of signature per step; the workflow settings of a space list and add meanings; the person dialog of the administration enables and disables signing.
- **The published copy (PUB-731, spec 073).** A twin of a signed version shows its signatures in the release band, whatever the setting of approvers; no record is copied. The export block of a twin is for PB4 (DC4-21).
- **Guide and pack (DOC-790).** `docs/operations/regulated-use.md` and `docs/operations/validation-pack.md`, with a test that every line of 09.8 is in the pack and every test it names exists.

## Load

The budgets of spec 064 for this wave are signing within 50 ms plus the confirmation, and verifying on display as a comparison, not a hash of the page (DOC-1303, DOC-1306). Measured through the API on the development database, five signed approvals and a hundred reads each, while the machine ran other builders' gates at a load of 20 to 25:

- **Signing:** the step that signs (taking the confirmation, hashing the version, writing the record, its audit entry and the approval) took 22 ms as the median and 26 ms at most, against 50 ms allowed. The confirmation itself is not counted, as the budget says: it is an Argon2id hash of the password.
- **Display:** reading a signed controlled page took 10.5 ms as the median, against 10.6 ms for a controlled page without signatures: the signatures ride in the query the band already makes.
- **The hash:** `BenchmarkSignedContentHash` hashes the canonical form of a document of 2000 paragraphs, about 170 KB, in 0.31 ms (548 MB/s).
- **Queries:** a page with signatures costs no query more; the history one more for the signatures of its versions. A full verification costs three small queries per signature and runs only on demand.
- **Not measured:** the real-data dump of wave PF was not loaded for this wave, so DOC-1306 is not re-measured there; the auditor's export of a large space.

## Checks

- **Tests first, in part.** The refusal tests of `internal/app/control_signatures_test.go` cover the wrong person, a role, a guest, a token, another person's confirmation, a stale login, an unconfirmed, reused, expired or misdirected confirmation, a password without a code, a passkey without user verification, an old login at the provider, a changed version, a removed reviewer, a failed step and tampering. They were written after the first code of the step they test and before that code had run once, not before it; their first run failed on most of them and found three faults, all fixed: a version row cannot be updated (the hash moved to `version_hashes`), the return path of a refused provider login overwrote the refusal, and the verification of a version without a kept hash failed instead of answering "does not match".
- **Failure paths.** Every test starts from a refusal before the success it allows. The export test changes one word of an exported document and sees the outside script fail.
- **`make check`:** passed on main `6038d62` with this wave on top, with the sharded race run.
- **Boundary:** five new tables have owners; identity's read of `user_identities` and pages' two new reads of `users` are listed with their reasons. Pins and their hashes are read through `attachments.PinnedFiles`; the trash and attachments ask the functions `holds_signed_records` and `signed_file_versions` of migration 0069.
- **Migration lint:** the index of audit entries of pages is built without `CONCURRENTLY`, with its exception written on the statement: only the sealer writes that table.
- **Targeted mutation check:** `MUTATION_JOBS=2` with every changed path outside `internal/app` and the script, on main `6038d62` with this wave: 559 of 1198 ran, 556 were caught. Three were not, all of this wave: "a confirmation counts twice" was hidden by the replay test, which changed the version before replaying; "another person's confirmation signs" removed a check that the session check repeats, since a session belongs to its person; and "the trash purges signed records" did not compile. A test now replays a confirmation on the same step of the same version, a test uses a confirmation from another session of the same person (the mutation now removes the session check), and the third mutation compiles. All three were then run again by hand, the same way, and caught. 21 mutations are new.
- **E2E:** `control.spec.ts` and `0-design.spec.ts` on the rebased tree. The new pictures `signing-dialog` and `signed-band` were looked at; `workflow-editor-phone` changed because the list of workflows is taller now (it ends with the meanings of signatures), so the click on Change scrolls further down; the editor itself looks as before. `template-versions` (spec 074, a screen this wave does not touch) differed by the width of its date column between runs, also before this wave's pictures were made, and was left as it is on main.
- **Web:** tsc, eslint, prettier, strings, design and vitest pass.

## Not proven in this wave

- The signing login against a real identity provider: the Go tests use the test provider of `internal/app`. The suite against Authentik needs the image of the release, which this wave did not build.
- The restore drill's signature check runs on a drill without a signed page, so it proves the command, not a signature that survived a restore. `TestTamperingInTheDatabaseTurnsTheMarkToDoesNotMatch` proves what the command checks.
- A passkey in a real browser for a signature: the Go test signs with a key made in the test, with and without user verification; the browser tests sign with a password and a code.
- The budgets of DOC-1306 on the real-data dump: signing and display were measured on the development database (above), not on the dump.
- The PDF report of the auditor's export, and the export as background work (DC5).
- The signatures in the PDF and Word exports of a published copy (PUB-731, PB4).
