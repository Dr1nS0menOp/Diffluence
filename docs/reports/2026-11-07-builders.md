# Builder reports, 6 and 7 November 2026

What the builders of these two days reported beyond their specs and handoffs. Read with `docs/operations/limits.md`.

## How the work went

- Up to three builders ran at once, each in its own worktree and compose project. No crash. Heavy gates (`scripts/e2e`, the mutation check) waited for each other and for at least 3 GB of free memory in WSL.
- The mutation check takes over an hour. Its first mutations run whole test packages and take minutes each; later ones run one test and are fast. It is the slowest gate, so builders take turns.
- Twice a builder changed the main checkout through a relative path: a debug line in `web/e2e/helpers.ts`, and three files that were restored. Builders must use absolute paths. Look at `git status` on main before landing.
- `go test -race` needed 20 minutes on GitHub runners, because `internal/app` alone takes over 7 minutes locally.
- Tests that pressed keys right after a menu closed failed on slower runners. Wait with `editorSettled(page)`, or retry a key that means the same each time.

## Self-test of blueprint 04 (spec 025)

- The test ran with its own limits on the target. During the gates, the limits were looser than agreed (8 CPUs and 6 GB, 4 CPUs and 4 GB).
- One request from a helper container reached `example.com` to prove that helpers could reach the internet. The rule was no internet traffic, so this broke it once; images were also pulled.
- Fixed: a Markdown paste that kept the server busy for minutes, helpers that could reach the database and the internet, a lockout anyone could cause, one oversized event that stopped the queue, and very large messages to the editing service.
- Not tested: several nodes, NATS cluster, PgBouncer, S3, Helm, 30 editors, 5,000 simulated people, N and N+1 under load, restore, Collabora, draw.io, frames in a real browser, a backlog over days, DNS rebinding, and `compose.yaml` itself started.

## Search and the tree (spec 028)

- Before, under the model of 5,000 people: 95 percent of searches and reads failed. After: none failed, search p95 44 ms.
- The database needs about 2.5 CPUs at that model; 1.5 was too tight.
- Engines compared: Meilisearch about 2 ms a search, OpenSearch 7 to 17 ms, tuned PostgreSQL 10 to 14 ms. Both engines keep a copy that can show a page right after a permission change hides it. Search stays in PostgreSQL (ADR-037).
- Still slow: a common word filtered to comments only (678 ms), a wildcard inside a word (219 ms). The dialogs for Move, Content, export and Delete still load the whole tree.
- Not proven: real servers, four times the search load, a standby, a cold cache.
