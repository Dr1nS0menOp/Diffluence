# Wave RV9: labels of releases, reading under page restrictions, and the job for later joiners

Builder report for three follow-ups that wave DC5 named. It covers what was found, built, measured and proven.

## 1. Labels written by a release

**What was looked at.** Every writer of page labels, and whether each goes through the rule of DOC-402 for exempt labels (`space.workflows` to put one on, take one off, or rename onto or away from one):

| Writer | Path | Before this wave |
|---|---|---|
| A person, the API | `pages.AddLabel`, `RemoveLabel` | the rule |
| Renaming in a space | `pages.RenameLabel` | the rule |
| Template sets (GT1 to GT3), label rules included | `pages.CreateSet` through `addLabel` | the rule |
| Markdown import | `exchange` plan through `pages.AddLabel` | the rule |
| Automation | `automation` actions through `pages.AddLabel`, `RemoveLabel` | the rule, as the rule's actor |
| The example space | `example` through `pages.AddLabel` | the rule |
| Copy of a page or branch | `pages` tree copy, `dropExemptLabels` | exempt labels its maker may not set are dropped |
| Bundle import | `exchange` bundle import, direct insert | a new space whose importer gets the space administrator template, `space.workflows` included, with the source's control rows as they were |
| Release (PB1 to PB3) | `pages.WriteTwin`, `setReleasedLabels` | the draft's labels were written onto the twin as they were |

**What a release did.** A published space holds no workflow of its own by PUB-703: making one there is refused. But linking checked only that the published space was empty of content, so a space whose space workflow (or a page workflow on its home page) had been set before it was linked kept it in force after linking, and so did a published space that was linked again after an ended pair. In that shape its twins were governed by that workflow, and a release copied labels onto them without the rule and without settling the page again, so the twin's labels and its control could disagree until something settled it.

**What changed.**

- `pages.requireExemptRights` is the one function for the rule: of the labels a change puts on or takes off, it answers those exempt in the space and refuses the change when there are any and the caller lacks `space.workflows` there. `requireExemptRight` (one label) and `RenameLabel` use it.
- `setReleasedLabels` works out which labels the release puts on the twin or takes off, asks `requireExemptRights` in the twin's space, and settles and audits each exempt one through `followExemption`, as a person's change does. A release that would set or remove an exempt label is refused, since nobody holds `space.workflows` in a published space.
- Linking refuses a published space that follows a workflow, on the space, on a page, or through a controlled page (`pages.FollowsWorkflow`), in the preview and in the link, also when the same two spaces are linked again (`published_space_controlled`).
- Not changed: copy keeps dropping exempt labels its maker may not set (DC5-10); the bundle import keeps writing its labels directly, as its importer holds `space.workflows` in the new space and the control rows arrive as the source had them.

## 2. Who is asked to confirm reading

**Before.** The groups of a read-and-understood state were resolved by `space.view` in the page's space, at approval and in the job. Someone a view restriction keeps out of the page was asked, could not open or confirm the page, and was listed as not confirmed.

**Now.** A new permission function `perm_page_readers(page, people)` (migration 0073) answers which of a list of people may view one page: the rule of `perm_visible_pages` asked per page for many people, with the view restrictions read once for the page and its ancestors and the control rule read only for a page never approved or withdrawn. Approval and the job both resolve through it. The permission matrix test compares it with the literal rule for every case it builds, beside `perm_visible_pages`.

`perm_visible_pages(u)` for each member was tried first and measured (below): it works out every page each member may see, which with 1,950 view restrictions in the installation took 4.4 s for a group of 5,000; `perm_spaces` plus `perm_hidden_pages` took 3.7 to 5.3 s for the same reason. `perm_page_readers` took 0.11 s.

## 3. The job for people who join later

**Before.** Each minute the job `control-due` joined every open reading request with every member of its groups and looked for a missing confirmation, so its cost grew with requests times members whether anyone had joined or not.

**Now.**

- A trigger on `group_members` notes every join in `read_joins`, in the join's own transaction, so a join becomes visible to the job exactly when it commits, whatever order transactions commit in. Every join is noted, not only joins of groups a request names now, because a request being made while the join is not yet committed could not see it.
- A pass takes up to 500 noted joins (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) and asks, through `perm_page_readers`, those still in the group, members, active and allowed to read the page, for each request whose current approved version names the group (found through a new GIN index on the groups of requests). The passes go on while a pass takes a full batch, as before.
- Approvals that ask for reading take a shared advisory lock and the job takes it alone. Without it, a join that commits after an approval's snapshot but before the approval commits would be invisible to the approval, and its request invisible to a job running in between.
- The migration notes the members of every group a request names, so the first pass after the upgrade asks once anyone the previous job had still to ask.

**Load, measured** on the development database (5,000 members in one group, 40 approved pages asking that group, 200,040 confirmations, other builders' gates running):

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| A pass with nobody new | 4.5 to 9.3 s, every minute | 2 to 4 ms for the whole job `control-due` |
| 100 new members | in the same scan | 0.14 to 0.27 s |
| All 5,000 members noted again at once | in the same scan | 4.0 s, once |
| Resolving the group at an approval | 0.11 to 0.18 s by space only | 0.11 s by page, with 1,950 view restrictions present |

The cost of a pass now follows the joins since the last pass times the requests that name their groups. A membership change adds one small insert.

## How the tests prove it

| Test | Proves |
|---|---|
| `TestASpaceThatFollowsAWorkflowIsNotLinkedAsThePublishedSpace` | A space workflow, a page workflow, and a workflow set after an ended pair each stop the preview and the link; with the workflows off the space links |
| `TestAReleaseCopiesLabelsUnderTheRuleForExemptLabels` | In a published space that exempts a label, a release that would take it off or put it on is refused and the twin keeps its labels; an ordinary label beside a kept exempt one is copied; no exemption changes |
| `TestOnlyThoseWhoLookAfterWorkflowsTouchAnExemptLabel` (unchanged) | The rule for a person, through the shared function |
| `TestOnlyPeopleWhoMayReadThePageAreAskedToConfirmIt` | At approval and for later joiners, someone a view restriction keeps out is not asked; the list holds only those who may read the page |
| `TestTheJobAsksEachLaterJoinerOnceAndOnlyAfterTheyJoin` | A pass with nobody new asks nobody; someone who joined and left before the pass is not asked; someone in two groups of one request is asked once; a later joiner is asked once over two passes and told once |
| `TestPeopleWhoJoinTheGroupLaterAreAsked` (unchanged) | A later joiner is asked and a guest is not, through the new job |
| `TestMatrixEvaluate` (extended) | `perm_page_readers` gives the literal rule's answer for every page and person the matrix builds |

## Seen failing

## Decisions taken on the product owner's behalf, may be overruled

- RV9-1: linking refuses a published space that follows a workflow rather than turning its workflows off: nothing is taken away without the person who looks after them doing it.
- RV9-2: a release that would put on or take off an exempt label of the published space is refused rather than leaving the label behind, so the twin never differs from what was released.
- RV9-3: the job asks people who join a group later, as DOC-1418 says. Someone already in the group who gains access to the page later, through a grant or a restriction lifted, is no longer asked by the job; the next approved version asks them.

## Not proven

- The race the advisory lock closes was reasoned about, not reproduced: it needs a join to commit inside the few milliseconds between an approval's resolution and its commit.
- Pairs linked before this wave whose published space already follows a workflow are not changed by the upgrade; their releases now refuse exempt labels of that workflow.
- The load was measured on synthetic data, not on the real-data dump.
