## What was built

- **The model.** `internal/workflow` is pure: states with a kind, transitions, a validator that names the broken rule, and the list of changes between two versions. Migration 0051 adds:
  - `workflows` and `workflow_versions`;
  - `space_workflows`, which holds the kinds the space workflow governs;
  - `page_workflows`, for one page or a branch;
  - `page_control`, one row per controlled page;
  - `workflow_rounds` and `workflow_answers`.
- **What readers see.** Once a page is approved, its `pages` row keeps the approved version. Every reader's route therefore shows approved content without asking: the page, the tree, search, links, exports, public sites. A change is written as a newer version, the head, that only the page's authors see.
  - A page that was never approved, or was withdrawn, exists only for its authors. This is one rule in `permissions.Evaluate`, and the same rule in `perm_control_hidden_pages` inside `perm_visible_pages` and `perm_listed_pages`.
  - Public sites, the feed, the history block, the contributors list and the version history also leave out versions readers may not see.
- **Review starts again.** Every route that writes a version goes through `appendVersion`: publishing (silent too), a rename, a restore, a ticked task, a copy. On a controlled page each of these moves the page to the start state, ends the open round as superseded, and opens a new round under the newest workflow version. The move is recorded as the system's, with its cause.
  - A version equal to the approved text returns the page to its approved state.
  - Approval makes the head current: search, links, blocks, mentions and tasks follow, and watchers hear of it.
- **Steps.** `POST /pages/{id}/transitions` names the version the person looked at. It is refused when the page moved on or a newer version arrived, and when a required comment is missing. It locks the page row, then the control row, in the same order as a publish.
- **Who governs.** The space workflow comes first, for the kinds it names. Then a page workflow on the page, then the nearest branch workflow above it.
  - Setting or removing one runs inside one transaction. The preview is the same change rolled back, so its counts are the change's own: approved, start, kept, leaving, and revealed.
  - New pages, moves and kind changes settle again.
- **Screens.**
  - A band under the title shows the state, the approved version, who approved it, why this workflow governs the page, and the steps on offer to its authors.
  - The space settings list the workflows.
  - Making and changing workflows goes through the API in this wave.

## Results on the real data

The dump of wave PF was used, with a tenth of the pages put under control: 5,962 pages, of which 1,142 are hidden from readers. `docs/operations/limits.md` has the tables. In short:

- **Open a page.** A controlled page as a reader takes 8.0 ms, against 6.4 ms for an ordinary one. As an author it takes 7.5 ms, against 6.7 ms.
- **An approval** takes 12.1 ms (median), 15.8 ms at most.
- **Tree, search and feed.** The tree takes 17 ms, search 28 ms, the recent feed 28 ms.
- **The visible set.** For the member it went from 26 ms to 39 ms. It is still built once per query as a hash. `TestTheFeedKeepsItsHashOnRealData` checks the plan.
- **For you, watcher of every page.** It went from 226 ms to 390 ms, within its budget of 500 ms. The other feeds are unchanged.

## Decided on the product owner's behalf on 2026-11-13, may be overruled

1. **Who takes steps.** In DC1, whoever may edit the page takes the steps of its workflow. Reviewers and who must answer come with DC2.
2. **Who sees what readers don't.** The working version, and a page readers do not see, are seen by those who hold the edit permission of the item's kind and are not kept out by an edit restriction. Archiving does not take that away.
3. **History.** A reader sees only the approved version in the history of a controlled page; authors see every version. A version that waited for review when control was removed stays out of a reader's history until the next publish (DOC-141).
4. **What follows the approved version.** Search, links, tasks and mentions follow it. Mentions in a working version are told when it is approved. A version under review never shows in the feed, the history block or the contributors.
5. **Kinds.** The kinds of item belong to the space workflow setting, not to the workflow itself. A page or branch workflow governs pages, blog posts and databases.
6. **Space over page.** Turning on a space workflow removes every page workflow of the space, also on kinds it does not govern.
7. **One workflow per page.** A page workflow is refused under a space workflow, and inside a branch workflow.
8. **Moving into a branch.** A page moved into a branch joins in the start state. If it was never approved under control, readers stop seeing it until it is.
9. **Removing control from a page readers did not see.** A page that was never approved, or was withdrawn, becomes an ordinary page that shows its current text. The preview counts these pages as "revealed" before anything changes.
10. **No background work.** An assignment runs in one transaction. Background work for very large spaces is left to a later wave.
11. **The space's approval rule.** On a controlled page it does not apply: the workflow decides.
12. **Copies.** A copy of a controlled page is made from the version readers see.
13. **System steps.** These are the restart, the return to the approved text, and an assignment. They are recorded with no person, under the actor "workflow", with the person whose change caused them as `caused_by`.
14. **Who sees workflows.** Anyone who views the space sees its workflows, so a person who sets one on a page can choose.
15. **Limits.** A space has at most 50 active workflows. A workflow has at most 30 states and 100 transitions. Ids are 1 to 40 lower-case letters, digits, `-` or `_`.
16. **Returning to the approved text.** The newest, equal version becomes the approved number; when and by whom stay those of the approval.
17. **Migration 0050.** It holds its number for wave HF, because the loader needs contiguous numbers. It is to be deleted when HF's 0050 lands.
