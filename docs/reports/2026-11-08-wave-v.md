# Wave V: carrying the load

Report of the build of spec 031: ARC-197, ARC-233 to ARC-236, ARC-238 and ARC-239 of blueprint 04.8.

## What was built

- **An order of work** (`internal/httpapi/admission.go`). Every request is sorted before its first query: saving (not GET), reading (GET), and search and reports (search, exports, insights, analytics, care, audit). With a pool of `n`: saving may use `n`, reading `n - n/4`, search and reports `n/4`, which counts as reading too. A class waits a short time (15 s, 3 s, 1 s) with a limit on how many wait (8n, 4n, 2 × its share); past that the answer is 503 `busy` with `Retry-After` at once. Probes and `/metrics` never wait.
- **Readiness.** `/readyz` says `busy` when every connection of the pool is taken (checked before the ping, which would otherwise fail as `database`), or when saving or reading has more than half its limit waiting. `/livez` is unchanged.
- **Password hashing** (`internal/identity/password.go`): at most half the cores hash at once per node, at least one.
- **A memo of permission facts per read request** (`internal/permissions/memo.go`): the person and the space are loaded once per request instead of once per check. A write gets no memo, so a grant it makes counts for the checks after it.
- **A query counter** on the pool (`internal/db`), also the metric `diffluence_db_queries_total`, and a budget test for the main screens (`internal/app/query_budget_test.go`).
- **A section *Load budget*** in the spec template.

## Measurements

The numbers are in `docs/operations/limits.md`, section "An order of work under too much load". In short: under a flood of page views, the node before served 378 a second with reading p95 2.7 s and saving p95 417 ms, and `/readyz` said ready throughout. After, it served 599 a second, refused 30% of reads at once, kept the rest at p95 303 ms, and saving p95 at 186 ms; `/readyz` said busy. No save failed in any run.

The first measuring run is not used: `make check` ran on the same machine during the "before" half, and the flood runs gave no result because the load tool refused more pre-allocated users than its maximum. Both were fixed and everything was measured again.

## What is left

- Search still has its own share inside `internal/search` as well as the class in the middleware. Folding the inner one away was left so as not to collide with wave T, which works in that package.
- The classes count requests, not connections. A handler that holds two connections at once is not seen; the pool still caps the total.
- The tree and search keep their query counts; their budgets are their current counts.

## Not proven

- Real servers, several nodes behind a load balancer, and what a balancer does when every node says busy.
- Floods longer than 40 seconds, and a cold database cache.
- Whether the screen shows the busy message well everywhere: it shows the message of the error, as for any refusal, and no screen was changed.

## For the product owner

- When every node says busy at once, a load balancer that only uses ready nodes has none left. `limits.md` says what to set. The alternative is a `/readyz` that never reports load, which gives up ARC-235.
