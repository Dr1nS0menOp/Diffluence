# 09.3: Reviewers, rules to pass, independence and delegation

How to read this chapter: see [01-document-control.md](01-document-control.md).

A review is only worth something if the right people did it, independently, on the exact text, and if the record shows who did what. ISO 9001 7.5.2 c asks for review and approval for suitability and adequacy; EU GMP Annex 11 clause 2 asks that responsibilities are defined; Part 11 §11.10(g) asks for authority checks, so that only authorised people can take each step. This chapter turns those into rules for who is asked, how many must agree, and what happens when people are away or leave.

## 3.1 Who is asked

- **DOC-200** · built · A state of kind *in review* names its **reviewers** as any mix of people, groups and roles of the space (the roles of wave R, AR and FU). *Why:* organisations assign review by function ("quality lead"), not by name, so that review survives staff changes (Annex 11 clause 2). *Edge cases:* a role with nobody in the space and a group with no members make an empty list; a round that would start with no reviewers is refused, and the author is told which entry was empty. *Proof:* tests for each kind of entry and for the empty case.
- **DOC-201** · built · Groups and roles are **resolved when the round starts**, and the resolved names are stored on the round. Joining or leaving the group later does not change who was asked. *Why:* the record must show who was responsible at the time (Part 11 §11.10(e)); a review list that changes afterwards would rewrite history. *Edge cases:* a group of 500 is resolved to 500 reviewers; with the rule "any one", that is sensible, with "all" it is refused when it exceeds a limit (decided: 50 reviewers per round under "all" or quorum, DOC-1408), because nobody can review with 500 sign-offs. *Proof:* a test that a later group change leaves the round unchanged; a test of the limit.
- **DOC-202** · built · The author can **add** reviewers to a running round, if the state allows it, and the addition is audited. Nobody can silently remove a reviewer: removal (as today, `RemoveApprover`) needs `space.workflows` or the reviewer's own "I cannot review this", and is audited with a reason. *Why:* removing a dissenting reviewer to pass a round is the classic way to defeat review. *Edge cases:* removing a reviewer who already answered "changes" does not undo the effect of that answer (DOC-211). *Proof:* tests that removal needs the right and a reason; a test that a "changes" answer stays effective after the reviewer is removed.
- **DOC-203** · built · A reviewer must be able to **read the working version** of the page while the round runs, even if a page restriction would otherwise keep them out. Being asked grants read access to that page's working and approved versions, and to the round, for the duration of the round only. This extends today's `approval.read`. *Why:* a reviewer cannot review what they cannot see; today's rounds already work this way. *Edge cases:* the grant never extends to child pages, attachments of other pages or the space; after the round ends, the reviewer keeps access only to the record of the round (PAG-065). *Proof:* permission matrix cases for a restricted page with a reviewer from outside the restriction.

## 3.2 Rules to pass

- **DOC-210** · built · A review state has a **rule to pass**: *all* reviewers approve; a **quorum** of N approve; or *any one* approves. The rule is shown on the review panel with the count so far ("2 of 3 needed"). *Why:* different documents need different assurance; ISO 9001 leaves the method to the organisation, but it must be defined. *Edge cases:* a quorum larger than the number of resolved reviewers is refused when the round starts. *Proof:* table tests for each rule and count.
- **DOC-211** · built · One answer **"changes"** ends the round under any rule, and moves the page to the state the workflow names for changes (back to draft, or to a state of kind *rejected*). *Why:* approving over an open objection is what review exists to prevent (decided, DOC-1402). *Edge cases:* a reviewer may change "approve" to "changes" until the round ends, which ends it; changing "changes" to "approve" is not possible after the round ended, because the round is over. *Proof:* a test per rule; a mutation that ignores a "changes" answer under quorum must be caught.
- **DOC-212** · built · A reviewer may change their answer **until the round ends**, as today (PAG-064). Every change is audited with the old and new answer. *Why:* Part 11 §11.10(e): the trail must not hide earlier values. *Proof:* an audit test that both answers are in the trail.
- **DOC-213** · built · When a round passes, the system takes the transition to the next state at once, in the same transaction as the last answer. *Why:* a gap between the last answer and the transition would leave a page approved in fact but not in the record. *Proof:* a test that the answer and the transition share one transaction, by failing the transition and checking the answer is not stored.

## 3.3 Independence

- **DOC-220** · built · A review state can require **independence**: the author of the change under review cannot count as a reviewer, even if they are on the list through a group or role. On by default for states that lead into an approved state (decided, DOC-1403). *Why:* the four-eyes principle that auditors expect for approval of controlled documents. *Edge cases:* the "author of the change" is everyone who published a version since the last approved version, not only the last one, so a colleague cannot write a change and have the original author approve it while they approve the colleague's; with "any one" and a single reviewer who is also an author, the round cannot pass and the panel says why. *Proof:* tests for one and several authors; a mutation that counts the author must be caught.
- **DOC-221** · built · An instance administrator can not switch independence off for a single round; only the workflow (with `space.workflows`) can, and that change is audited and versioned. *Why:* an exception must be a change of the procedure, not a quiet override. *Proof:* a test that no API route turns independence off per round.

## 3.4 Due dates, reminders and escalation

- **DOC-230** · new · A review state can set a **due period** (for example 5 working days). The round shows the due date. Reviewers are reminded a set time before it, and on the day. *Why:* reviews that never end leave readers on stale content. *Edge cases:* working days follow the instance's working week setting (decided: Monday to Friday unless set, DOC-1409); holidays are not known and the guide says so. *Proof:* tests with a fixed clock across a weekend.
- **DOC-231** · new · When the due date passes, the state does what the workflow says: remind again, **escalate** to a named person, group or role (who is added as a reviewer and notified), or move to another state that is not of kind *approved*. *Why:* escalation is how organisations keep review moving without lowering its standard. *Edge cases:* escalation never adds the author (independence); escalating twice adds the target once. *Proof:* tests per action; a test that a time-out can never approve (DOC-105).
- **DOC-232** · new · Due dates are computed in the instance's time zone and stored as moments in time. A change of the server clock or of daylight saving time never fires a reminder twice or skips it, because the job claims due rounds by their stored moment, once (09.13). *Proof:* a test that moves the clock back across a due moment and checks one reminder.

## 3.5 Delegation and absence

- **DOC-240** · new · A reviewer can **delegate** one round to another person who could be a reviewer (meets the same role or group, or is chosen by someone with `space.workflows`). The record shows "delegated by A to B" and the reason. The delegate's answer counts, the delegator's no longer. *Why:* absence is normal; Annex 11 clause 2 asks that responsibilities stay clear when people change. *Edge cases:* delegating to the author is refused when independence is on; chains of delegation are allowed but recorded in full. *Proof:* tests for each case.
- **DOC-241** · new · A person can set an **absence** with dates and a stand-in. Rounds that start while they are absent ask the stand-in instead, and the round records why. *Edge cases:* a stand-in who is themselves absent is skipped to their stand-in, at most three steps, then the round asks the escalation target. *Proof:* a test of the chain and its limit.
- **DOC-242** · new · A reviewer who is **disabled or removed** from the instance while a round runs is dropped from the round, with an audit entry, and the rule is recalculated. If that makes the round impossible (quorum no longer reachable), the round escalates. *Why:* a departed employee must not block a document for ever, and must not be able to answer after leaving. *Proof:* a test that a disabled reviewer cannot answer and that the round escalates.

## 3.6 What reviewers see

- **DOC-250** · built · The review panel shows the change **since the last approved version** by default (not since the previous version), with the usual compare view, the reviewers, their answers and comments, the rule and count, the due date, and any signature requirement. *Why:* reviewers must judge the whole change that readers will receive. *Proof:* a test that the compare base is the approved version.
- **DOC-251** · built · Reviewers can leave **review comments** that belong to the round, visible to reviewers and authors only, and inline comments on the working version (09.6). *Proof:* visibility tests for readers, authors and reviewers.
