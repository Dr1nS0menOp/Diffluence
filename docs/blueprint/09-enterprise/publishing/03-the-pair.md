# 09.P3: The pair: linking two spaces

How to read this chapter: see [01-draft-and-published-spaces.md](01-draft-and-published-spaces.md).

A pair is a lasting link between two spaces of one installation, with a direction: from the draft space to the published space. Inside the pair, each page of the draft space that was ever released has one twin in the published space. This chapter says how a pair is made, what it makes of the two spaces, what pairs with what, and how a pair ends.

## 3.1 The model

- **PUB-200** · new · A **pair** links one draft space to one published space (decided, PUB-1402). It holds who made it and when, its settings (PUB-240), and whether it is active or ended. A space is in at most one active pair, on one side; a pair from a space to itself is refused. *Why:* one source for every published page gives one answer to "where do I change this?"; forbidding a space to be both draft and published forbids chains, which could loop and could carry a draft through a middle space. *Edge cases:* an ended pair stays on record (PUB-245); a space can be in a new pair after its old one ended. *Proof:* tests that a second pair on either side, a chain and a self-pair are refused, with the reason.
- **PUB-201** · new · A **page pair** links one page of the draft space to its twin in the published space, with the draft version last released, the published version it became, when, by whom or by which approval, and the state of each other aspect at that release (chapter 04). A page pair is made by the first release of the page and lasts until the page is taken off (PUB-440) or the pair ends. *Why:* the twin must be found again on every release; matching by title would guess wrong, because titles are not unique. *Edge cases:* a draft page that was taken off and released again gets a new twin, and the old twin's history stays in the published space's trash until purged. *Proof:* tests of the first release, a second release, and release after take-off.
- **PUB-202** · new · A **file pair** links one file of a draft page to its copy on the twin, with the file version last copied (PUB-320). *Why:* files are copied only when they changed (PUB-1419), which needs to know what was copied. *Proof:* a test that an unchanged file is not copied twice.
- **PUB-203** · new · The published space's pages are **ordinary pages** in every read path: the tree, search, links, exports, the API and sites read them as they read any page. Only the write paths know the space is managed (PUB-220). *Why:* the read paths are the most used and the most tested; adding nothing to them adds no leak and no cost (12). *Proof:* the query-count tests of ARC-239 are unchanged for a page of the published space.

## 3.2 Making a pair

- **PUB-210** · new · A person with `space.administer` in **both** spaces links them, from the draft space's settings. The step has a preview: the published space will become managed (no one can edit it by hand any more), who will lose the ability to edit there (counted by people and groups, named up to 50), whether the draft space is readable by people who can read the published space (PUB-611), and that public sites and public links of the draft space will be withdrawn (PUB-1417). It is applied only after confirmation, in one transaction, and audited. *Why:* the change takes rights away; the person must see that before it happens (the same rule as DOC-140). *Proof:* a test of the preview's numbers; a test that a refused link changes nothing.
- **PUB-211** · new · The published space must be **empty** when linked: no page other than its home page, a home page with no version after its first and no files, no blog posts, folders or databases (decided, PUB-1404). Two ways make one: **"Make a published space"** creates a new space for the pair, with a name and key the person chooses and no view grants except the person's, so nothing is visible to readers until someone grants it; or the person picks an existing empty space. *Why:* matching an existing space's pages to the draft's would guess; an empty start is exact. *Edge cases:* an existing space with content becomes a published space only by starting a draft space from it (14, PUB-1302). *Proof:* tests that a non-empty space is refused with what is in it, and that a new published space starts with no reader.
- **PUB-212** · new · Linking needs the experimental part `publishing` to be on (PUB-1415, chapter 14). *Proof:* the 404 `experimental_off` test of spec 069 extended to every route of this feature.
- **PUB-213** · new · The two **home pages** are paired when the link is made (decided, PUB-1418); the published home page receives the draft home page's content at the first release. *Why:* readers start at the home page. *Proof:* a test.
- **PUB-214** · new · Linking makes **no release**. The first release is a separate act (chapter 05), and the release screen then lists every page as "not released yet". *Why:* a link and a release are different decisions; the first release of a large space deserves its own preview. *Proof:* a test that linking leaves the published space empty.

## 3.3 The managed published space

- **PUB-220** · new · In a published space, these actions are refused for everyone, whatever their roles, including instance administrators: creating pages, blog posts, folders, links and databases; editing; renaming; moving within, into or out of the space; copying into it; archiving and unarchiving; deleting, restoring from trash and purging pages; adding, replacing and deleting files; adding and removing labels on pages; setting statuses, workflows, approval rules, templates and automation that write pages; importing; and scheduled publishing (decided, PUB-1403). The page shows no Edit button and no write actions, because a control without permission is absent (design standard, States). *Why:* a published copy that someone edits by hand is either overwritten by the next release, losing the edit, or kept, and then the two sides drift and the pair means nothing. *Edge cases:* a person trying through the API gets 409 `managed_space` with a message naming the draft space only if they may view it, else "This space is changed only by releases." *Proof:* a matrix test of every write action against draft, published and ordinary spaces, for an instance administrator, a space administrator, an editor and a reader.
- **PUB-221** · new · What stays allowed in a published space: viewing, comments, reactions, watching, favourites, sharing, exports, page restrictions and access (`space.manage_access`, `restriction.manage`), the space's own settings (name, description, look, shortcuts, the animal), public sites and public links (`site.publish`), analytics, and the audit view. *Why:* these concern readers, not the content; the published space's administrator decides who reads (PUB-1405). *Proof:* matrix cases.
- **PUB-222** · new · Restrictions set on a published page **stay** across releases; a release never adds, changes or removes one. *Why:* who may read a page in the published space is decided there. *Edge cases:* a restriction on a published page also applies to the pages released below it, as view restrictions inherit (ADR-009); the release screen shows the draft page's twin as "restricted for some readers" to those who may see that. *Proof:* a test that a second release keeps the restriction.
- **PUB-223** · new · Reader comments on a published page stay on that page, and the draft page shows its authors how many there are, with a link, when they may view the published page (decided, PUB-1429). *Why:* readers' questions are what writers most need to hear. *Proof:* tests that the count shows only to those who may view the published page, and that no comment text crosses.
- **PUB-224** · new · A **database trigger** refuses inserts and updates of pages, versions and attachments in a published space unless the transaction marks itself as a release (decided, PUB-1430). *Why:* during an upgrade an older node does not know the rule (14), and a new write path added later could forget it; the trigger holds either way. *Edge cases:* the mark is a transaction-local setting the release path sets; the app role can set it too, so the trigger protects against mistakes, not against a compromised application, which the permission rule and the audit cover. *Proof:* a test that an insert without the mark fails, with the message naming the rule; a mutation that drops the trigger must be caught.

## 3.4 What pairs with what

- **PUB-230** · new · **Pages**, **folders** and **links** (the kinds) are released. A folder's twin is a folder; a link's twin is a link to the same address. *Why:* the tree's shape is part of what readers see. *Proof:* tests per kind.
- **PUB-231** · later · **Blog posts** are not released in spec 073; they stay in the draft space, and the published space has no blog. *Why:* posts are dated news with their own feed, which a release model must treat differently (when a post appears is its date); a later wave adds it if teams ask. *Proof:* a test that a post is never in a run.
- **PUB-232** · not · **Databases** are never released in spec 073 and are listed as "cannot be released" with the reason. *Why:* databases are an experimental part (ADR-043) whose rows change constantly; releasing a snapshot of a database is a different feature. A page that embeds a database view shows "not available here" in the published copy (PUB-523).
- **PUB-233** · not · **Templates** of the draft space are not released; the published space has none, since nobody creates pages there.
- **PUB-234** · new · **Personal drafts** (a page never published in the draft space) and unsaved edits are never released; a release takes the draft page's current version, which is always a published version of the draft space. *Why:* a release can only show what the draft space's own members can already read as a version. *Proof:* a test that a page with an unsaved edit releases its last version.

## 3.5 Settings of a pair

- **PUB-240** · new · A pair has these settings, kept on the draft side and changed by those with `space.administer` in the draft space, each change audited: *who may release* (shown as the holders of `space.release`); *release on approval* (08, off until a workflow is set); *mirror removals in whole-space releases* (on by default, PUB-1410); *notify watchers* (on by default); and *the default release note*. *Proof:* tests per setting.
- **PUB-241** · new · The draft space shows, in its settings, a **health line** for the pair: whether people who can read the published space can also read the draft space (PUB-611), whether any file failed to copy, how many pages are out of step and since when. *Proof:* tests per line.

## 3.6 Ending a pair

- **PUB-243** · new · Ending a pair needs `space.administer` in both spaces, or in the draft space alone when the published space was deleted (PUB-1046). It has a preview: the published space becomes an ordinary space that its editors can change again, the draft space stays as it is, and how many pages are out of step and will stay so. *Why:* ending is the way back; it must be possible while the part is off too (PUB-1425). *Proof:* tests of the preview and of both spaces after the end.
- **PUB-244** · new · Ending a pair is refused while a run is going (PUB-1421). *Proof:* a test.
- **PUB-245** · new · An ended pair, its page pairs and its runs stay on record, readable in the audit of both spaces. Linking the same two spaces again **restores** the page pairs whose two pages both still exist, and marks every page by comparing again (chapter 04); the published space need not be empty then. *Why:* an administrator who ended a pair by mistake must be able to go back without starting over. *Edge cases:* a page edited in the published space while the pair was ended is out of step with its draft, and the next release overwrites the edit; the preview of linking again lists such pages. *Proof:* a test of relinking after edits on both sides.
- **PUB-246** · new · Deleting either space ends the pair (11.6). *Proof:* tests.
