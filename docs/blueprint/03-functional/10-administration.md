# 03.10: Administration

## 10.1 The console

- **ADM-001** · built · **Administration** in the account menu opens the console, with a vertical menu.
- **ADM-002** · built · The sections are users, groups, global permissions, spaces, settings, SCIM tokens, audit log and system health.
- **ADM-003** · built · The console has a search over settings, so an operator finds things by name.
- **ADM-004** · built · The first page is a summary: number of users, spaces, pages and files, storage used, failed jobs, and things that need attention.
- **ADM-005** · built · Sensitive changes ask for the password or passkey again, even in a running session.
- **ADM-059** · new · Replaces the way in of ADM-001: an administrator reaches the administration from the settings button at the end of the top bar, between help and their account picture, on every screen (NAV-095), and the account menu no longer holds Administration. A person who may not use the administration does not see the button (NAV-004) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-060** · new · The sidebar of the administration starts with a header row: a back action that leaves the administration for the start screen, and the name of the area in bold. The header stays in place while everything under it scrolls, a thin line under it appears only once the list has scrolled beneath it, and the back action shows a quiet fill under the pointer. The settings of a space have a header of their own (SPC-136) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-061** · new · Under the header the sidebar has two tiers. First the tool sections, under bold headings without a fold control, always open, each entry an icon followed by its name; then, after extra space, a bold **Settings** heading one step larger than the group headings, the jump field of ADM-003 under it, and the settings groups that fold. A thin line divides the sidebar from the large area (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-062** · new · A settings group has a smaller bold heading with a boxed fold arrow before it, pointing down when the group is open and towards the end side when it is shut. Its entries are text only, compact, and indented so that they start in the same column as the names of the icon entries above. Groups are set apart by space, not by lines; several may be open at once; folding one leaves the large area as it is; and what is open or shut is kept per person from section to section and across reloads, as the parts of the space sidebar are (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-063** · new · The jump field belongs to the settings part, under its heading, instead of above the whole sidebar (spec 013 F9). It has a search mark at its start and a visible border at rest.
- **ADM-064** · new · The sidebar scrolls apart from the large area, with its own scrollbar over its full height. Only the header row stays; the Settings heading and the jump field scroll away with the groups. Moving from one section to another keeps the sidebar at the same scroll position, and it moves only when the person scrolls it. Whether the current entry is brought into view when a section is opened from an address or a search result needs its own study.
- **ADM-065** · new · The entry of the open section, an entry under the pointer and keyboard focus in the sidebar of the administration look as NAV-144 and NAV-145 say.
- **ADM-066** · new · An entry that leads out of the instance, such as one into the documentation, ends with an outward mark at the end of its row, all such marks in one column, and screen readers hear that it opens elsewhere. Entries inside the administration have no such mark. A name that does not fit ends in an ellipsis on one line and shows in full on hover and on focus.
- **ADM-067** · new · A section that came with the running release carries the **New** mark of NAV-178 straight after its name in the sidebar, and the section of an experimental part that is on carries an **Experimental** mark (ADR-043): a word in a lozenge, in sentence case, never colour alone, which keeps its look in the selected row.
- **ADM-068** · new · Beyond ADM-002, the tool part of the sidebar holds three sections in this order. The first, **Overview**, holds Summary (ADM-004), Insights (PLT-014), Analytics (PLT-001), Audit log (section 10.8), System health (section 10.9), Events, Upgrades and Configuration (OPS-116) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-069** · new · The second tool section, **Tools**, holds in this order: Automation (chapter 12); Access check (ACC-166 to ACC-168), for what one person or group may reach across the instance (beyond ACC-069); Permission export (ACC-156), for the whole instance; Spaces (section 10.6); Announcements, which takes the site banner of ADM-017 out of Look into a section of its own; and Import from other tools (phase 11). A tool for moving from separate grants to roles is not offered, because roles exist and existing grants became roles without anyone gaining or losing access (ADR-034) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-070** · new · The third tool section, **People and access**, holds Users (section 10.2), Groups (ACC-124), Invitations, where the requests of ACC-041 wait for approval (ACC-139, ADM-120), and Service accounts (ACC-140), which leaves the Users section for an entry of its own. They are ordinary entries of the administration, with icons and without an outward mark, because the instance manages its own people (ADR-008); there is no billing entry, because nothing is paid (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-071** · new · The settings groups follow in this order. The first, **Instance**, holds General, which is today's Settings section, renamed because Settings now heads the whole settings part; Rules (section 10.4); Organising, with the features and the experimental parts (ADR-043); Mail (section 10.5); Link shortcuts (EDT-207); Templates (section 10.7); Document control (09-enterprise); PDF font (EXC-069); and Code blocks (EDT-218) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-072** · new · The second settings group, **Security**, holds Security overview (ADM-125); Login and second factor, which takes the login settings out of General (ACC-013 to ACC-019); Sessions; Provisioning (ACC-044); Export and publishing, in the place of a section for public links, with the public sites and single shared pages that replace them (EXC-038, EXC-080); and Outgoing calls (ADM-024). There is no section for reading without an account (Where we deliberately differ 1) or for another product's help desk, and teams, once they exist (ACC-130), go into People and access (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-073** · new · The third settings group, **Permissions**, holds Instance permissions (ACC-148); Roles (ACC-152), for the space roles, the instance roles and the roles the product gives by itself (ADM-158); Access in many spaces at once (ADM-167); Defaults for new spaces (ADM-023), out of Rules into an entry of its own; Access to all spaces (ADM-165), where an administrator also recovers access (ADM-031) and sees who holds which role in each space (ACC-160); Access requests (COL-049, COL-111); Guests (ACC-035 to ACC-042, ACC-133); and, from phase 7, App permissions (PLT-099). A guide for moving to roles is not offered (ADR-034) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-074** · new · The fourth settings group, **Look**, holds Look (ADM-013 to ADM-016, ADM-018), in the place of a section for the navigation bar; PDF design (EXC-056), for the look of exported PDFs across the instance; Header and footer (ADM-147); and Watermark (ADM-150). There is no choice of whole themes, because there is one look (ADR-034), and no default space picture, because every space has its animal (ADR-031) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-075** · new · The fifth settings group, **Data**, holds Use of dynamic blocks; Space reports; Care (section 10.10); Backups (OPS-115); Import a space, for bundles (EXC-072); Spaces in the trash (ADM-032), out of Spaces into an entry of its own; Linked instances (PLT-049), in the place of links to other applications; Webhooks; and Other tools menu (NAV-100). There is no repair tool for another product's blocks (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-076** · later · From phase 7 two more settings groups end the list. First a group for finding apps and managing the installed ones, where managing lists each installed app with its version, its state and the rights it was given (PLT-039). Last the group **Apps**, with the settings sections that installed apps add (PLT-041): each named after its app and at its own address, plain indented text without icons, in alphabetical order, looking exactly like the built-in entries, and one app may bring several, such as a first-steps section and its settings. Such a section runs in the app's sandboxed frame on the app's own address, so it cannot reach the session of the administration.
- **ADM-077** · new · Every section sits in exactly one group of ADM-068 to ADM-076, which replace the six groups of today (Overview, People and access, Content, Automation, Look, System). A section added later goes where its kind belongs: something to watch, such as the activity of tokens, into Overview; a tool that acts, such as AI (phase 6), into Tools; people into People and access; and screens about sign-in, data protection and allowed network addresses into Security (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-078** · new · The large area of every section holds one column of reading width, centred between the sidebar and the far edge of the window with equal empty margins, with generous space between title, text, notices and tables, and the page ends after its last part. A list whose columns need more room, such as the list of automation rules or of all spaces, starts beside the sidebar and takes the full width. Nothing runs past the window: a wide table scrolls in its own box. For the administration, and through SPC-150 for the settings of a space, this replaces the Look item of ADR-038, "settings and the administration stay at the left beside their sidebar" (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-079** · new · Every section is built in one arrangement: the centred column, labels above their controls, hints under them, the file chooser of the components and one primary action. A screen that repeats a part, such as one block per kind of backup, gives each the same pattern: heading, text, controls, action. Labels beside their fields with a colon, status lines in italics, the browser's own file chooser and a form pressed against the sidebar without a margin are not used.
- **ADM-080** · new · Every section starts with its title, the first and largest text on the screen and the same as its name in the sidebar, with no breadcrumb or tabs above it. The actions of the screen sit at the end of the title row: the one primary action, a secondary action after it (NAV-164), then a More button for actions used rarely. Under the head follow, in this order, the introduction (ADM-081), notices, tiles or filters, the count line and the content; the largest gap of the screen lies under the head.
- **ADM-081** · new · The introduction of every section of the administration is the description of NAV-113.
- **ADM-082** · new · A part of a section has a bold heading one step smaller than the title, the same size on every screen, and may carry its own action at the end of its heading row, such as adding to the list under it. A rule about a list, such as that only one item can be active at a time, is a muted line just above that list.
- **ADM-083** · new · A section that holds more than one list shows them as tabs under its introduction: text only, the chosen tab in accent with a bar under it, a hairline across the full width under the row, and no counts. Each tab opens with a heading that repeats its name, with that list's main action at the end of the heading row, then one sentence, then the list at full width; the title row then carries no action.
- **ADM-084** · new · A notice inside a section of the administration is the note of NAV-149 and NAV-150, as wide as the column.
- **ADM-085** · new · A notice about the whole section stands under the introduction and above the filters; a notice about one part stands right before that part or inside it. A notice about a setting or a lasting cause has no close button and stays while the cause lasts, and the cause also shows in each place it touches, such as a table cell or the security overview. A notice of news is dismissed as NAV-150 says, and several notices stack with a small gap in the order they came.
- **ADM-086** · new · Beyond NAV-160, in a settings form the controls stack at the start side of the column; a checkbox has its label beside it; radio options stack closely; a number field has its unit or a short trailing word on the same line after it; and a part of the form may be headed by the question it asks, with more room above that heading than below it.
- **ADM-087** · new · A hint stands in smaller muted text directly under its control, starting under the box rather than under the label. The hint of a group of options stands between the group's label and the options, and a hint about one option is indented under that option. Links in a hint are underlined, a hint may send to another section of the administration by such a link, and a link that leaves the instance carries the outward mark (ADM-066).
- **ADM-088** · new · A required field of the administration is marked as NAV-165 says.
- **ADM-089** · new · An information mark after a label, a column heading or the title of a tile is the explanation button of NAV-152.
- **ADM-090** · new · A settings form of the administration saves as one unit, with Save and Reset changes, as NAV-162 says.
- **ADM-091** · new · A section whose parts take effect apart holds one unit per part, each with its own Save, as NAV-163 says.
- **ADM-092** · new · How a setting changes depends on its kind. A table of who may do what is read-only, without actions per row, until its one Edit button, at the end of the row above the table, turns the whole table into choices with Save and Reset changes (NAV-162). A form edits in place (NAV-162), and a single switch, or a role chosen in a row, applies at once with a short message that says what changed (NAV-161, spec 043 D7).
- **ADM-093** · new · A switch of the administration looks and is labelled as NAV-166 says.
- **ADM-094** · new · Beyond NAV-171, a button that starts something stays inactive until its form is complete, a file is chosen or the earlier steps are done, and a line beside it says what is still missing, as NAV-004 allows. This is a state of its own beside busy and no permission (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-095** · new · A sentence of warning, with its key words in bold, stands directly above a button whose action is hard to undo, at the start side, and the button still asks before it acts.
- **ADM-096** · new · Settings or tasks that belong together may stand as rows in one card with a thin outline and rounded corners, with a divider between the rows. Each row has an optional icon tile at its start, a bold name, one muted line of description and its control at the end edge, so the controls form one column; a control is centred on the text, or level with the name in a taller row (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-097** · new · An image setting, such as a logo, uses a drop zone with a dashed outline and centred content: the current image, a remove action in the zone's top end corner, a link that opens the file chooser, and words saying that a file can also be dropped there. Every drop zone of the administration has the same outline.
- **ADM-098** · new · A file that starts a job, such as a space to import, is chosen with a button that carries an upload mark; the chosen file's name then shows beside it, and the screen's start button follows under it (ADM-094).
- **ADM-099** · new · A choice between a few kinds that each need explaining shows them as cards side by side, each with its radio at the top start, a bold title beside it and a muted description of about two lines under the title. All cards keep the same light outline, and the chosen one shows by its filled radio and its accessible name.
- **ADM-100** · new · A value in a range, such as a percentage, is set with a thin slider, filled up to its knob, paired with a small number field at its end. The two stay in step, both work from the keyboard, a mark on the slider shows the maximum, and the number fields of a form line up with the end of the column.
- **ADM-101** · new · Beyond NAV-170, a setting that the configuration file or the environment fixes shows its value read-only, with one line in words that says where it is set, file or environment (OPS-100, OPS-116). Save acts only on what can be changed, a screen never shows a locked control without saying why, and when a setting needs something done elsewhere first, a notice before it says what (ADM-085).
- **ADM-102** · new · A read-only setting keeps its control in view in the muted, inactive look of NAV-170: a box with its tick, a field with its number, radio options with the chosen one. Only the control and its own label turn muted, its hint keeps normal text, and this read-only state differs from no permission, where the control is absent (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-103** · new · A long list has one tool row above it: first a search field with its search mark at the start and a stronger border, which filters as one types; then one button per filter, each an icon and a name of the same height, which opens a small chooser; then a button for more filters when there are many; and the screen's own action, such as Edit, alone at the end edge of the row. A filter that is set shows its name and value in words and the active look of accent outline, accent text and tint, the others keep a neutral outline, there is no Filter button because a choice applies at once, and the filters are part of the address (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-104** · new · A list that has a few states, such as active and archived spaces, offers them as a row of rounded pills above the tool row, the chosen pill marked for screen readers as well as by its look.
- **ADM-105** · new · Between the tool row and the list, a muted count line says how many rows match out of how many there are, counting only what the person may see (NAV-006), with a refresh button after it that loads the list again and keeps the filters. Whether an exact total stays cheap on a very large list, or only says that there are more than a limit, is open (needs its own study).
- **ADM-106** · new · Figures that sum up a list stand as a row of equal tiles across the column, between the introduction and the list. Each tile has a small title with an information mark (ADM-089) that says how the number is counted, and a large bold number, without unit, trend or date.
- **ADM-107** · new · A table of the administration follows NAV-157, with compact rows and cells centred vertically (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-108** · new · The first column of a table holds what a row is about and is the widest, from a third to three fifths of the table. It may hold two lines, the name over a muted line of description, may start with an icon tile and end with a lozenge, and the name is a link when the row opens something. Narrow columns of choices stand side by side towards the end; a mark for granted or not granted stands at the start of its column and has words for screen readers; numbers are aligned to the start; and a state is a word with a mark, never colour alone.
- **ADM-109** · new · Row actions sit in the last column, aligned to the end, which has no visible heading but a name for screen readers: a single action is a quiet text button, a destructive one still asks first, and further actions sit behind a More button of three dots. The pointer over a row tints the whole row.
- **ADM-110** · new · A list that can grow long sorts by its columns, and the sorted column's heading shows accent words and an arrow. A list whose rows can be chosen starts with a column of checkboxes. Paging shows under the table only when the rows do not fit. In a list of parents and children a parent row is bold and its children are indented, so the structure does not rest on weight alone.
- **ADM-111** · new · A switch inside a table row is centred under its heading and applies at once (ADM-092), and that heading may carry an information mark straight after its words. A second line in a cell is muted, and when it is an address that leaves the instance it is underlined and carries the outward mark.
- **ADM-112** · new · A short list of entries made of a name and an address, such as the other tools menu (NAV-100), is edited in place. Its first row is an add row: empty fields under the column headings, which also name the fields for screen readers, the other choices of a row, and a small primary Add in the action column. Existing rows read as plain text, their choosers without a border until touched but lined up with the add row's fields, and each row has a settings button. Lines run only under the heading, under the add row and after the last row, and every change applies at once, without a Save for the table.
- **ADM-113** · new · The order of such a list changes by dragging a handle in a gutter before the table's start, and also by Move up and Move down in the row's menu, so it works without a mouse.
- **ADM-114** · new · An empty list of the administration keeps its heading row with one muted sentence centred under it and no picture (NAV-172). When the whole section is empty and the person may fill it, the empty block of NAV-173 holds its introduction, its documentation link and its main action, and the title row then holds no button.
- **ADM-115** · new · Beyond NAV-174, a section that cannot work because something an administrator can add is missing, such as an optional service that is not installed, stays in the sidebar. It shows its title at the top, then a column centred on the large area with one of our icons, a heading smaller than the title, two lines on what the section does and what it needs, a link to the documentation, and one primary action when the person may take it; there is no illustration and nothing to buy. An experimental part that is off stays hidden instead (ADR-043) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-116** · new · Beyond ADM-004, the summary holds under its title, in this order: a row of figures (ADM-117), a row of shortcuts (ADM-118), three cards about outside access, experimental parts and administrators (ADM-119), the list of things that need attention, and then, after a rule and under a section heading about activity, the cards of waiting invitation requests (ADM-120), of active people (ADM-121) and of spaces over time (ADM-122). Every card ends with a link to the screen where its matter is managed, and the figures that ADM-004 names stay.
- **ADM-117** · new · The row of figures is one wide card with three figures side by side, aligned to the start and without lines between them: the number of spaces, of pages and of searches, each a caption, the value and a mark of its trend against the previous period. The number of searches shows only while analytics are on (PLT-001), without names. How long the period is, and how the figures stay cheap to count (ARC-238), is open (needs its own study).
- **ADM-118** · new · The shortcuts are a row of wide, low outlined buttons under a small bold heading, each an icon and a word centred, for the most common tasks: invite people, create a group and create a space. They are not primary buttons, and the summary has no primary button.
- **ADM-119** · new · Three equal cards stand in a row, each a caption, up to four rows of a name and its value, and a link at its bottom start. Outside access counts the guests, the single shared pages and the public sites, and links to Guests; Experimental parts lists each part with whether it is on, and links to Organising (ADR-043); Administrators shows each one's picture and name, and links to the list of all of them. There are no rows for public links into the instance or reading without an account, because neither exists (Where we deliberately differ 1).
- **ADM-120** · new · A card shows how many requests for an invitation wait (ACC-041), with a button that opens them for review in Invitations (ADM-070, ACC-139).
- **ADM-121** · new · A full-width card shows the number of people active per week over the last four weeks, counted from logins rather than page views, so it works with analytics off and names nobody. A button chooses another period and another returns to the default; which periods are offered is open (needs its own study).
- **ADM-122** · new · The last card shows the number of spaces over time: its caption, a link to the insights about spaces in its top end corner, the total and its trend, and a chart with a few grid lines, a zero line and ticks, in which team spaces and personal spaces are two series that differ by shape as well as colour, named in a legend under the chart (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-123** · new · A card on the summary has a bold title, a small outlined action at its top end (view, review, refresh or More), a large figure under the title, which is the total of the chart's sentence, a muted caption, and then the chart. A chart of parts, a ring with its total in the centre and the legend beside it or a stacked bar with the percentage in each part and the legend under it, may hold several parts when its legend names each with its number; a chart of one series has no legend and no axis titles (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-124** · new · A value that is not known yet shows as a dash, which screen readers hear as not known, rather than as zero.
- **ADM-125** · new · A **Security overview** opens the Security group: the steps that make the installation safer, in groups, each with a state the product works out by itself (done, needs attention, not started), one line on why it matters, a count where it helps, and a link to the screen where the step is done. Nobody ticks a step by hand, the overview changes nothing by itself, and nothing asks an outside server (SEC-900). How an administrator sets a step aside on purpose, such as a small team without an identity provider, is open (needs its own study).
- **ADM-126** · new · The steps of the security overview are: at least two active administrators; an identity provider for login and provisioning (SEC-162, ACC-044); a second factor required for administrators and for members (ACC-015, SEC-164); a passkey held by every administrator (SEC-162); rules for login and for guests; the data controls looked at; the public address on https (SEC-257); two database roles (ADR-019); encrypted backups, a backup noted recently and a tested restore (OPS-077, OPS-115); the audit chain checked lately and its witness lines kept outside the host (ADM-041, SEC-833); and, once apps exist, rules for apps. Steps that only serve a vendor of many organisations, such as proving a domain or choosing a data region, are not offered.
- **ADM-127** · new · A step that needs attention also shows among the things that need attention on the summary (ADM-004, ADM-049). Under the steps, four shortcut cards lead to the figures about account security, the audit log, the list of tokens, and the documentation at the address the administrator chose (NAV-068).
- **ADM-128** · new · On the security overview each group starts with a small bold heading outside its rows, with more room above it than between rows. Each step is a full-width row with a quiet outline and a small gap to the next, and the whole row is one link: a square tile at its start shows the state by tint and symbol, also said in words to screen readers, then a bold title, one softer line, sub-steps as bullets when there are any, and at the far end a small outlined count with a unit word before an arrow; in a tall row the tile, count and arrow stay at the top, in a short row they are centred. The four cards under the groups are equal, each with an icon tile, a bold title, two lines and a secondary button; the screen has no primary button and ends after the cards (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).

## 10.2 Users

- **ADM-006** · built · An administrator lists users with search, opens one, and changes name, type, permissions and status.
- **ADM-007** · built · Disabling a user ends their sessions at once. They can no longer log in by any method.
- **ADM-008** · built · The list filters by type, status, group, last login and how they log in.
- **ADM-009** · built · Bulk actions: disable, add to group. Import of users from CSV for instances without SCIM.
- **ADM-010** · partly · A user's screen shows groups, spaces, last login, and pages they own, and offers to hand over ownership. Built: last login, groups, the spaces they can open and how many pages they own, without titles (spec 030). Still open: hand-over of ownership.
- **ADM-011** · built · A list of people who have not logged in for a chosen time helps to clean up.
- **ADM-012** · built · Administrators cannot read content through user screens.
- **ADM-129** · new · Beyond ADM-006, the Users screen shows four tiles of equal width between its introduction and the tool row (ADM-106): all accounts of every type, people who logged in during the last 30 days, accounts that come from the identity provider, and administrators. The tiles are figures, not buttons, and those for all accounts and for the identity provider carry an information mark that says how they count (ADM-089); they show counts only, never names.
- **ADM-130** · new · Beyond ADM-008, the list of users also filters by role and by space, and its search finds a person by name or e-mail address. In the tool row (ADM-103) type, role and group stand as filter buttons after the search, and More filters adds status, space, last login and how people log in.
- **ADM-131** · new · The list of users has, in this order, a column of checkboxes for the bulk actions of ADM-009, the person, the widest column, with picture, name and the e-mail address muted under the name and the mark for a guest or a service account beside it (`PersonMark`), then status, last login, and a row menu under a heading that only screen readers hear. Rows come in name order, and the list pages when the rows do not fit (ADM-110) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **ADM-132** · new · A user's status is a word with a mark (ADM-108): active in the success colour, invited and not yet joined in a neutral one, disabled, or guest account ended (ACC-039). Last login is relative time (NAV-078), or a muted word that says never, and its heading carries an information mark that says what counts as a login (ADM-089); what the row menu holds needs its own study.

## 10.3 Look of the instance

- **ADM-013** · built · An administrator sets the instance name and logo, shown in the top bar and on the login page. The file is checked like any image.
- **ADM-014** · built · An administrator chooses an accent colour. The app checks contrast in light and dark, and refuses a colour that fails.
- **ADM-015** · built · An administrator imports a WOFF2 or WOFF font, which replaces the system font, and removes it again. It is served from our own address.

The columns are name, with a mark for a token that has ended, scope, owner, last use, and end date, with a mark for a token that ends within 30 days, then Revoke in each row; the list shows what is stored about a token (ACC-019, SEC-807), never its secret and never content (SEC-601).
- **ADM-195** · new · Export, a normal button at the end of the title row of Tokens, downloads the list as CSV. The tool row holds a search by token name and filters for state, scope, owner and last use that act at once (ADM-103), the count line with its refresh follows (ADM-105), and the list sorts on last use, shown as relative time with tokens never used first, and on end date, shown as a date.
- **ADM-196** · new · An administrator revokes one token, the tokens chosen through a column of checkboxes with one that chooses all, or every token of one person, with a reason and a fresh proof of identity (ADM-005). The owner is told and each token is audited as revoked; whether tokens that ended stay listed or are removed needs its own study.
- **ADM-197** · later · The administration has one screen of **data controls** in the Security group (ADM-077): the rules that decide how content may leave the instance or be reached from outside. It lists export formats (EXC-017), downloading files (FIL-053), public sites and single shared pages (EXC-038, EXC-044), bundles (EXC-009), webhooks (PLT-038), and apps and the AI server once they exist (PLT-039, PLT-047); there is no control for reading without an account, because the instance offers none (deliberate difference 1), and once it exists it takes the place of Export and publishing (ADM-072).
- **ADM-198** · later · Each row of data controls shows the control, with an icon tile and its name as a link that opens its setting; its default, allowed or blocked, as a word with a mark; where it acts, every space or chosen spaces as chips; how many spaces differ from the default; who changed it last and when, or the word default while nobody has; and a row menu. The introduction says that a control can turn a capability off entirely or limit it in finer steps, such as to groups.
- **ADM-199** · later · Data controls has two tabs, active and draft (ADM-083), so that a change to a control can be prepared as a draft that acts only once it is turned on; what a draft is for, a start date or a preview of what would be refused, needs its own study, and there is no feedback button (SEC-900).
- **ADM-200** · later · An optional scan looks through new versions, comments and text files for what looks like a secret or personal data: private keys, passwords beside a user name, tokens with a known prefix, Diffluence's own among them, and bank and card numbers. It runs in the worker on new content only, as an experimental part that starts off (ADR-043), and each hit becomes a **finding**, listed in the section Findings of the Security group (ADM-077).
- **ADM-201** · later · A space administrator sees the findings of their space with the page and the passage, while an administrator of the instance sees counts per space and kind and pages by space key and id (SEC-606), unless they recover access (deliberate difference 2). The owner of the page and the space administrators are told of a new finding, and no finding or message ever repeats the secret itself.
- **ADM-202** · later · A finding is marked as fixed or as no problem, which is audited, a new finding can start a rule (PLT-025), and a secret in an old version is removed with that version, with a reason (PAG-096). The patterns and their false hits, old versions and the trash, how long findings are kept, and whether the check before publishing (SEC-384) shares the patterns need their own study.
- **ADM-203** · new · For system administrators (PRM-005), a section Encryption in the Security group shows how stored data is protected: what the application encrypts, files and the secrets of the installation (SEC-725), and what relies on disk and backup encryption, the text of pages (SEC-726, SEC-636). It shows where the key lives, the id and age of the current key, and when keys were last replaced and last checked, each with the command or the guide for that step (SEC-713), and it never shows a key.
- **ADM-204** · new · The section Encryption is read-only: keys are replaced on the command line (SEC-713), and there is no choice of who holds the keys, because the operator always does. What `diffluence key check` can report to the screen without the screen holding a key needs its own study.
- **ADM-205** · new · A section **Compliance** in the Security group holds one card per standard that chapter 06.12 maps Diffluence to: OWASP ASVS, the OWASP Top 10, NIS2 as the Dutch Cyberbeveiligingswet, ISO/IEC 27002 with BIO2, and article 32 of the GDPR. A card shows the region the standard belongs to, its name, one line on what it asks and a link to the mapping in the documentation (NAV-068); the section claims no certificate, says that the mapping is no legal advice, and fetches nothing.
- **ADM-206** · new · The compliance cards stand stacked close under the introduction, each across the column with a quiet outline, rounded corners and a soft lower edge, holding close together and aligned to the start: the region in small plain text, the name in bold, the line of description at reading width, and the link (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
