# 03.12: Analytics, automation and the platform

## 12.1 Analytics: principles

Page views are personal data. They are not in the audit log (decided 2026-11-03), and they are a feature with its own switch.

- **PLT-001** · built · Analytics can be turned off for the instance or per space. When off, no views are recorded.
- **PLT-002** · built · **Privacy mode:** an administrator chooses between counts only (no names), or names for space administrators. The default is counts only.
- **PLT-003** · built · Moving to a stricter mode removes names from existing data. Going back never restores them.
- **PLT-004** · built · View data is kept for one year by default, and the period is a setting.
- **PLT-005** · built · A person's own views are left out of counts on their own pages.
- **PLT-006** · built · Reports never show anything about pages the viewer may not see.
- **PLT-007** · built · Who may see analytics is a permission per space.

## 12.2 Page insights

- **PLT-008** · built · Selecting the view count in the byline opens a summary: a chart of views and of unique readers over time.
- **PLT-009** · built · A period can be chosen: 7 days, 30 days, 3 months, this year, or a range.
- **PLT-010** · built · In name mode, a list shows who read it, when, how often, and which version they saw last.
- **PLT-011** · built · A reader counts as engaged after 20 seconds on the page, or as a brief visit.
- **PLT-012** · built · **Mentioned people:** whether people mentioned in the page have read it.
- **PLT-013** · built · **Links:** the most used links in the page, and the pages that bring the most readers here.

## 12.3 Space and instance insights

- **PLT-014** · built · **Instance:** active users, views, new pages, comments and searches over time.
- **PLT-015** · built · **Search insights:** the most used searches, and searches that found nothing, without names.
- **PLT-016** · partly · **Space:** popular pages, most active readers and contributors (name mode only), with full lists.
- **PLT-017** · built · A **content table** per space shows title, created, last changed, last viewed, comments, unique readers and views, and sorts on every column.
- **PLT-018** · built · A **people table** per space shows, in name mode, pages made, edits, comments and views.
- **PLT-019** · partly · Every table exports as CSV, and only active items are listed unless asked.
- **PLT-020** · built · **Stale content:** pages not viewed or changed for a chosen time, with a link to the Content screen (SPC-060).
- **PLT-051** · new · Instance insights are split into topics, one tab each, in a row under the screen's title and its one sentence on what the screen shows, with a help link: Activity (PLT-014), Search (PLT-015), People and Sign-in. The selected tab is marked by accent text and an underline only, the tabs carry no counts, and each topic has its own address.
- **PLT-052** · new · A topic is a stack of chart cards of the full width. Each card has a bold heading, one sentence under it that says what is counted and carries the total and the trend, a refresh button and a More menu at its top end, and the chart below them.
- **PLT-053** · new · A chart in insights has a bold title on each axis, muted tick labels, a light grid and a darker base line, and no numbers on its bars; the numbers are in its table (PLT-054).
- **PLT-054** · new · Every chart card says when its figures were counted. Refresh counts them again, and the result is kept for a short time, so pressing it often adds no load (ADR-036). The card's More menu shows the numbers as a table and downloads them as CSV, as the tables of PLT-019 do.
- **PLT-055** · new · **People:** a chart of accounts by kind, one bar each for members, guests, service accounts and disabled accounts, as counts without names, for the whole instance and with no period.
- **PLT-056** · new · **People:** a bar chart of active accounts by the domain of their e-mail address, from most to fewest, with every domain however small, so accounts from an unexpected domain stand out. A domain names no person, so the chart ranks nobody (SEC-934).
- **PLT-057** · new · **Sign-in:** how many active accounts have a second factor, a passkey, both or neither, how many sign in through the identity provider, and how many administrators have neither a second factor nor a passkey. The topic names nobody: to follow up, an administrator opens the user list filtered by how people log in (ADM-008), and a card on the first page of the administration (ADM-004) leads here.
- **PLT-058** · new · **Sign-in over time:** failed logins, accounts whose password login stopped after too many attempts (ADR-038), and tokens in use, per day, as counts that never rank or name people.

## 12.4 Automation

- **PLT-021** · built · A rule is a trigger, conditions and actions, built in a screen with no code.
- **PLT-022** · built · Rules exist per space, or for the whole instance when made by an administrator.
- **PLT-023** · built · A rule acts as a chosen service account or as its owner, and can never do more than that account may do.
- **PLT-024** · built · Rules run in the background through the queue.
- **PLT-025** · built · **Triggers:** page published, edited, moved, copied, archived, deleted, commented on, labelled, status changed, owner changed; blog post published, commented on, labelled; attachment added or removed; task created or ticked; person mentioned; person added to or removed from a group; space created or archived; on a schedule; by hand from a page; incoming webhook.
- **PLT-026** · partly · **Conditions:** in a space or below a page, has a label, has a status, title or text contains, created by, older than, member of a group, plus and, or and not.
- **PLT-027** · partly · **Actions on pages:** publish a new page from a template, copy, move, archive, delete, restrict, add or remove a label, change status, change owner, add a comment, add or remove watchers.
- **PLT-028** · built · **Actions for people:** send a notification, send an e-mail, assign a task.
- **PLT-029** · built · **Actions outward:** call a webhook, post to a chat system through a webhook.
- **PLT-030** · built · **Lookups and values:** a rule can find pages, and use values such as the page title and author in later steps.
- **PLT-031** · built · **Run log:** each run lists what it did, what it skipped and why. A failing rule is reported to its owner.
- **PLT-032** · built · **Limits:** a loop cannot run forever. A rule cannot trigger itself, and each rule has a maximum number of runs per hour.
- **PLT-033** · built · **Templates for rules:** common rules ship ready to use, such as "remind the owner of pages not updated for a year".
- **PLT-034** · built · Rules can be turned off, copied, exported and imported.
- **PLT-035** · built · Every action of a rule is audited with the rule's name.
- **PLT-059** · new · Beyond PLT-025, a rule can also start when a database is created, when a folder is created, or when a task is reopened.
- **PLT-060** · new · The attachment trigger shows in the list of triggers as four rows: added to a page, added to a blog post, removed from a page, removed from a blog post. The task trigger is one row for a task whose state changes, set to ticked, reopened or both.
- **PLT-061** · later · When live documents (OTH-001), whiteboards (OTH-006) and outside link cards (EDT-067) are built, each brings its triggers: a live document started, a whiteboard created, and a link card added or changed. A link card trigger exists only for domains an administrator allowed (ADM-024) and carries no content fetched from outside (deliberate difference 5).
- **PLT-062** · new · Beyond *by hand from a page* (PLT-025), a writer can put a rule button in the text, from the slash menu or the insert dialog: they pick a rule of the space and the words on the button.
- **PLT-063** · new · A reader who presses a rule button runs that rule on the page, as the rule's account (PLT-023) and within its limits (PLT-032), and a short message says whether it ran; the run is in the run log (PLT-031). The button shows only to people who may run that rule (NAV-004). While the rule is off or gone, readers do not see the button, and writers see a placeholder that says why.
- **PLT-064** · new · Beyond PLT-026, a condition can compare two values: a required first value, a comparison (equals by default), and an optional second value. A second value left empty tests whether the first is empty. Each value is typed text, placeholders (PLT-030) or both, and placeholders are filled in before the comparison.
- **PLT-065** · new · The comparisons for text are: equals, does not equal, starts with, contains, does not contain, contains a pattern, matches a pattern as a whole, and does not match a pattern. A pattern is read after its placeholders are filled in, runs on a matcher with guaranteed linear time and has a length limit, so no pattern can stall the queue. Comparisons for numbers and dates, and whether case counts, are not known yet (needs its own study).
- **PLT-066** · new · Under the form of a value comparison, a collapsible section explains which values can be compared and how a pattern comparison reads the second value, with an example sentence with placeholders and its filled-in result, and links to the help on values and on patterns.
- **PLT-067** · new · Beyond PLT-026, a page condition holds one or more criteria, which must all match. Each criterion picks a field of the page together with its comparison in one list, then takes a value in a field under it, and has its own remove button; a text button under the last criterion adds another. On the title the comparisons are those of PLT-065, with equals chosen by default. The other fields of a page and their comparisons are not known yet (needs its own study).
- **PLT-068** · new · A person condition tests one person against chosen people or the members of chosen groups. Its first list says which person is tested: the person who caused the trigger, or another person the trigger gives, such as the author or the owner; a people field (NAV-088) holds the chosen people. It has exactly one criterion, with no remove button and no way to add another. When one rule starts another, the person who caused the trigger is the first rule's account (ARC-055).
- **PLT-069** · later · A condition can be a search query: it matches when the page or blog post of the trigger is among the results, as the rule's account sees them (spec 015 D11). It uses the search syntax (ORG-028 to ORG-031), and DQL once that exists (08). Unlike ORG-032, a query that cannot be understood is an error beside its field, never a fallback to plain words.
- **PLT-070** · later · A rule can hold an if / else block of one or more parts, each with its own conditions and its own steps. The parts are tried in order and the first whose conditions match runs its steps; the steps after the block run whichever part ran. A part matches when all its conditions match, which is the default, or when at least one does, and *not* stays available as in PLT-026.
- **PLT-071** · new · Beyond PLT-030, a rule can hold a *for each* step: it takes a set of items, runs the steps inside it once for each item, and then the rule goes on with the steps after it. The ready-made sets are: pages not active for a chosen time, pages whose owner is disabled, items of a chosen kind related to the trigger's item, tasks, and pages found by criteria (PLT-073). Which items count as related to a page is not known yet (needs its own study).
- **PLT-072** · new · A set holds at most 100 items, as a lookup does, and the 50 actions a run may take count across all its rounds (spec 015 H6). A set holds only items the rule's account may see (spec 015 D11), and the set of inactive pages leaves out pages with children by default, as SPC-061 does.
- **PLT-073** · new · Pages found by criteria, in a *for each* step or a Find pages lookup (PLT-030), are chosen by rows of criteria that must all match; the first row asks for the authors, in a people field under it. Each row has its own remove button and a text button adds another. The criteria are those of the Content screen's filters (SPC-061), so a lookup and that screen find the same pages.
- **PLT-074** · later · A *for each* step can also run over the items of a list value, such as a list a webhook brings in, with the flows of the roadmap (08).
- **PLT-075** · new · Every field that accepts values has a button at its end that opens a value picker for that field. The picker has a search field and a filter by kind of value, lists the values that each earlier step supplies under that step's name, says which field a chosen value goes into, and puts the value's placeholder at the cursor. It offers placeholders only, never functions (spec 015 D13).
- **PLT-076** · new · Values exist for the space, the page or blog post, the comment (on the page or on words in it), the task, the attachment, the label, a person, and the person who started the rule, each with its own properties, and the help lists them all. A placeholder that does not exist is an error beside its field, and a value of something the rule's account may not see is never filled in (spec 015 D11).
- **PLT-077** · new · Each trigger states what it gives the steps after it: a page, a blog post, a space, a person or a task. A step that needs something its trigger does not give is marked on its card, and its settings say why and how to fix it: choose another trigger, or place the step inside a step that supplies what it needs, such as a *for each* over pages.
- **PLT-078** · new · A rule with a step that does not fit its trigger, or with a step that is not fully set up, can be saved switched off, but cannot be switched on until that is fixed (spec 015).
- **PLT-079** · new · Beyond its steps, a rule has a name (required), a description in a box of about five lines, and an owner (required), who is at first the person who makes it, shown with picture and name. A muted line under the owner says that the owner is told when the rule fails, and the description shows in the list of rules.
- **PLT-080** · new · The owner of a rule chooses how they hear about failures: at every failure, once when a rule that worked starts failing (the default), or not at all. No choice sends more than one notice per hour (spec 015 H6).
- **PLT-081** · new · Where a rule acts (spec 033), the account it acts as (PLT-023) and the settings of its trigger sit in the rule's settings, apart from its details; the details end with a note that says so, with a link there and a help link.
- **PLT-082** · new · The owner of a rule chooses who may change it, in a required list marked with a lock: only the owner (the default), or everyone who may manage automation where the rule lives. Others who may see the rule can still open it, run it by hand where allowed, and copy it.
- **PLT-083** · new · A space administrator can always switch a rule of their space off at once, also one that only its owner may change. An administrator can take such a rule over, with a reason, and that is audited, as Recover access is (ACC-070).
- **PLT-084** · new · A rule can carry labels, written as page labels are (ORG-035), so rules can be grouped; the labels show in the list of rules and filter it. Rule labels are a list of their own, apart from the labels of pages, so grouping rules never changes what a label search finds.
- **PLT-085** · new · The settings of every step except the trigger offer Remove, and Copy, which puts a copy with the same settings right after it; Help appears where that step has documentation. A rule always has exactly one trigger, which can be changed but not removed. A placeholder and an if / else block offer only Remove.
- **PLT-086** · new · Beyond PLT-033 and the templates of spec 015 H5, Diffluence ships templates that: give pages a new owner when their owner is disabled, on a schedule; archive pages not active for a chosen time, on a schedule; replace one label by another on every page, on a schedule; change the owner of a page when it is moved; change the restrictions of a page when it is moved; and label the pages that chosen people publish.
- **PLT-087** · later · When AI is set up (PLT-042), templates also: send by e-mail a summary and the action items of new meeting notes; add the label the AI finds best when a page is published; and offer to make a database when the AI finds that a new page holds a plan, goals or other data, which needs *database.create* (ADR-038) and databases turned on (ADR-043).
- **PLT-088** · new · Choosing a template opens a new rule, not saved yet, with its steps, name and description filled in and a mark beside its name that it came from a template. Steps that need a choice before the rule can work, such as which person or which label, are marked on their card in words and named in the description, and the rule cannot be switched on until they are filled in.
- **PLT-159** · later · When classification levels for content exist (chapter 3), a rule can also start when the level of a space, a page or a blog post changes.

## 12.5 The platform

- **PLT-036** · built · Our own frontend uses only the public API.
- **PLT-037** · partly · The API is documented, versioned and stable, with OAuth scopes.
- **PLT-038** · built · Webhooks send events to outside systems, signed, with retries and a delivery log.
- **PLT-039** · phase 7 · Apps run as sandboxed frames on a separate address, then as WebAssembly blocks. A marketplace comes last.
- **PLT-040** · built · A **command line tool** uses the API, for scripted import, export and backups of content.
- **PLT-041** · new · The Create menu, the page menu, the sidebar and dynamic blocks all have defined places where apps can add entries.
- **PLT-089** · later · Beyond PLT-041, apps can add entries in these places too: the slash menu and the insert dialog, the byline of a page, the More menu of a page, the global part of the sidebar, the sidebar of a space, the settings of a space, the administration, and the lists of triggers, conditions and steps of rules. The lines below say how each looks.
- **PLT-090** · later · An app's block shows in the slash menu and the insert dialog among Diffluence's own, in the category the app declares, with the app's icon, its name and a description, and nothing else that marks it as an app's; without a description the name stands alone. A block that an app keeps only so that older pages still work stays readable in those pages, but is not offered for new use.
- **PLT-091** · later · An app can add a short entry to the byline of a page, in reading and in the editor, after the owner's name. The entry is text and a link that the app gives, never its own HTML (PAG-122).
- **PLT-092** · later · The More menu of a page has an Apps sub-menu that holds the actions apps add to a page.
- **PLT-093** · later · In the global part of the sidebar, apps show under the Apps entry of NAV-129.
- **PLT-094** · later · In the sidebar of a space, apps show in the Space apps group of SPC-105 and SPC-160.
- **PLT-095** · later · In the administration, apps have the two settings groups of ADM-076: one for finding and managing apps, and **Apps** for the settings sections that apps add.
- **PLT-097** · later · An app can add its own section to the settings of a space, at the end of the group for connections to other tools, named after the app and looking like a built-in section, with no icon and no mark. It shows only to people who hold what that section needs (NAV-004).
- **PLT-098** · later · Apps can add triggers, conditions and steps to rules. They show in the lists of triggers and steps in a group named after the app, each row naming the app in words, and a rule that uses them still does only what its account may do (PLT-023). An app's event reaches the instance through the API, so the server fetches nothing by itself (deliberate difference 5).
- **PLT-099** · later · A section of the permissions group of the administration, its last, lists in a table what each installed app may do in the instance: the app, with its icon or its initials on a round tint and its name, in the first and widest column, then one column per instance permission an app can hold, such as create spaces, create a personal space and view profiles. Each cell is a mark with words for granted or not granted. Rows are in alphabetical order, with no sorting, paging, row menu or selection.
- **PLT-100** · later · The heading of that table counts the apps. Above it, a field narrows the list by name, a quiet button for filters narrows it by permission, and one Edit button at the end of that row makes the table changeable; until then it is read-only. An app holds at first what its consent screen showed when it was installed (RMP-021); an administrator can narrow that and choose which spaces and groups may use the app. An app can never hold *administrator* or *system administrator*, and never reads more than the person using it (deliberate difference 2).
- **PLT-101** · new · When a release marks an API route for removal (ARC-135), administrators see a warning notice in the administration: an icon, a bold heading that names the route and the date it stops, a line on what replaces it, and links to the details and to dismiss it. Each administrator dismisses it for themselves. The notice ships with the release, so nothing is fetched to show it.
- **PLT-102** · later · The notice also says whether tokens of this instance called that route lately, once that can be known without new load, since the record of token use (SEC-807) does not name the route (needs its own study).

## 12.6 AI

Later, and always optional (phase 6).

- **PLT-042** · phase 6 · An administrator chooses the provider, including a local model. Nothing leaves the instance without that choice.
- **PLT-043** · phase 6 · Summarise a page, a long comment thread or a changes since a last visit.
- **PLT-044** · phase 6 · Rewrite selected text: shorter, clearer, other tone, other language.
- **PLT-045** · phase 6 · Ask a question of the wiki, with answers that cite pages. Only pages the person may see are used.
- **PLT-046** · phase 6 · Suggest labels and titles, and extract tasks from meeting notes.
- **PLT-047** · phase 6 · An MCP server gives assistants access under the person's own permissions.
- **PLT-048** · phase 6 · Every AI action is audited, and can be turned off per space.
- **PLT-103** · later · When AI is on for the instance and the space, one floating button in the bottom end corner of the space home and of every page, below the page's other floating tools, opens the assistant beside the page. When AI is off, the button is absent. Whether the assistant shares the place of Details and comments, and whether the button shows in the editor, is not known yet (needs its own study).
- **PLT-104** · later · When AI is set up, a rule can have a condition that asks the chosen model a yes-or-no question about the page of the trigger, using only what the rule's account may read (PLT-045). The question and the answer show in the run log, the answer is a value later steps can use, and the condition does not match when AI is off for the space or the model cannot be reached.

- **PLT-050** · phase 8 · Spaces, users and history can be moved or merged between them, for example after a merger. Stable ids make that possible.

## 12.8 The Automation screen

How the Automation screen of space settings and of the administration is laid out, with its head and its four tabs; automation is experimental and off by default (ADR-043), so these lines apply where it is on.

- **PLT-105** · new · The Automation section of space settings and of the administration is absent while automation is off for the installation or the space (ADR-043, SPC-047). When on, it opens in the large area of the settings frame with a head line: the title at the start; at the end a link to the automation of the whole instance, in space settings and for administrators only, and then the one primary button, Create rule. The same head stays on every tab.
- **PLT-106** · new · Create rule is a split button: its main part starts an empty rule, and its arrow part offers starting empty or from a template (spec 015 H5). A More button after it holds import and export (PLT-034).
- **PLT-107** · new · Under the head sit four text tabs, Rules, Run log, Templates and Usage, with a hairline across the full width. The selected tab has accent text and an underline, and the tabs carry no counts. Arrow keys move between them, and each tab has its own address.
- **PLT-108** · new · Each tab starts with a sub-heading at the start of its own line, with the controls of that tab at the end of the same line, and below it a row of filters where the tab has one.
- **PLT-109** · new · A row of filters starts with a search field, followed by bordered filter buttons, each an icon and a name, each opening a small chooser below the row. An unset filter shows only its name; a set filter is tinted and shows its value after its name, and the filters are part of the address. Where a tab has them, a thin divider, a quiet text button that clears the filters, and a borderless More button end the row. A filter has the same name on every tab.
- **PLT-110** · new · The Rules tab lists the rules in a table with these columns in this order: name, labels, trigger, owner, where it acts, last changed, last run, an on/off switch, and a row menu. The name column is the widest and labels the next, the others are narrow, the switch is centred and the row menu comes last.
- **PLT-111** · new · The list is sorted by name, A to Z, by default, which an arrow on that header alone shows, and every header sorts. The list has no selection boxes, no count and no paging.
- **PLT-112** · new · A rule's name is a link that opens the rule and may wrap over two lines; a rule with a description has an explanation button by its name that shows the description on hover or focus. The owner shows as picture and name, also when it is a service account; where it acts as the space's badge and name, or, for a rule of the instance, every space or its chosen spaces as chips (spec 033); the last change as a relative time. An empty labels cell stays blank.
- **PLT-113** · new · The switch turns a rule on or off at once from the list, and shows a tick while on. A rule that cannot be switched on (PLT-078) keeps its switch off, and the reason shows beside it.
- **PLT-114** · new · The row menu of a rule holds copy, export and delete; delete asks first.
- **PLT-115** · new · Above the table, the row of filters (PLT-109) starts with a text field that narrows the list by part of the name, then filters for where the rule acts, owner, action, trigger and label. Where the rule acts is set by default: to this space in space settings, and to all rules in the administration.
- **PLT-116** · new · The list of a space also shows the rules of the instance that act in that space, with the instance as their place, read-only there (spec 033).
- **PLT-117** · later · When rules come in more than one kind, the list gets a kind column before the switch, showing the kind as an outlined word, and a filter by kind.
- **PLT-118** · new · The Run log tab lists the runs of every rule the viewer may manage, newest first, in a table with the date and time, the run's own number, the rule, its status, and how long it took in all; the first column leaves room for the button that opens a row. An opened row shows each step, what it did or skipped and why, and how long it took. Runs of a rule the viewer may not open stay out, and runs are kept 30 days (spec 015 H6).
- **PLT-119** · new · The row of filters of the run log has a field that finds a rule, with a list to pick one; a period, set to the last 30 days by default and showing that value; and filters for the run number, the status, the action and the trigger; then the divider, the button that clears the filters, and More. A line above the table says which rows are shown, out of how many.
- **PLT-120** · new · When no run matches, the table shows a centred muted sentence that says so. Under the table, a collapsible section explains each status of a run: done; nothing to do, because the conditions did not match; partly failed; failed; and paused at its limit (PLT-032).
- **PLT-121** · new · The Templates tab opens with a tinted band across the full width under the tabs, holding a bold heading and one sentence about starting from a template, with no picture and no action.
- **PLT-122** · new · Below the band, a search and three filters, by category, by trigger and by app (the last only once apps exist), narrow the library. The templates stand in sections, one per category, each under a bold heading without a count, such as keeping order and working with other tools.
- **PLT-123** · new · A section is a grid of equal cards with even gaps, as many to a row as the width allows, six on a wide screen. A card holds a row of two to four small icon tiles, the trigger first and then the actions, conditions left out, and under it a title of up to four lines that says what the template does, start-aligned, and nothing else. The whole card is one link that opens the template, and its name for screen readers says the trigger and the actions in words (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-124** · new · A template that needs an app, a connection or AI that is not set up is left out of the library (NAV-004).
- **PLT-125** · new · The Usage tab shows how much the rules did in a chosen period. At the end of its sub-heading line sit when the figures were last worked out, as a date and a time, then a list of periods, those of PLT-009, and a refresh button, both ordinary buttons. There is nothing to buy and no plan to change. In space settings it covers the rules of that space, in the administration every rule.
- **PLT-126** · new · Under that line stand two framed tiles side by side, of equal width, each with a bold title and an explanation button on its first line and its number on the second: the steps that ran, and the AI calls, the second only when AI is set up (PLT-042) (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-127** · new · Below the tiles, a framed card of the full width ranks the rules that did most, in a table: rule, steps (sorted highest first, which its header shows), AI calls, owner, on or off, and a narrow last column for the row menu; every header sorts. Each rule also shows its runs in the period and its most runs in one hour against its limit (PLT-032), so a rule close to its limit stands out before it pauses. Without runs in the period, the card holds one centred sentence that no rule ran.
- **PLT-128** · new · While the figures load, each number is a pale placeholder bar, so the screen keeps its shape; the bars are hidden from screen readers, and one loading status says what loads (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).

## 12.9 The screen of a rule

How a rule is built on its own screen, as a chain of steps beside a panel of settings; the behaviour of section 12.4 stays as it is.

- **PLT-129** · new · A rule opens on its own screen in the large area of the settings frame, while the top bar and the settings sidebar stay: a narrow header strip across the area, a canvas that fills the rest, and a settings panel that lies over the end side of the canvas, about a third of its width (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-130** · new · The header strip holds at its start a boxed back button to the list of rules, the automation icon and the rule's name in small regular text, with in words beside it whether the rule is on or off and whether it is saved; at its end the one primary button and then a boxed More button.
- **PLT-131** · new · The primary button is split: its main part saves the rule and switches it on, and its arrow part, set off by a thin line, offers saving it switched off. It stays unavailable until a trigger is chosen, and from then on is available, also while a later step is unfinished (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-132** · new · More on the screen of a rule holds turn off, copy, export and delete, and is there before the first save as well.
- **PLT-133** · new · A new rule starts with a default name, which can be changed at any time in its details. A rule without a trigger cannot be saved.
- **PLT-134** · new · The steps show from top to bottom as a chain of cards joined by thin lines, the trigger first, at the start side of the canvas, and the room between the chain and the panel stays empty. The cards have one fixed width, and a quiet pattern of dots marks the canvas behind them (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-135** · new · A card holds a small tile with the step's icon, a title and a second line, and nothing else: no handle, number, menu or switch. The kind of a step shows by its icon in a colour of its own, also on a placeholder, and the card's name for screen readers starts with the kind in words (When, If, For each, Then), so the kind never rests on colour (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-136** · new · Selecting a card opens its settings in the panel and gives the card an accent border. A wide pale halo is added only while the card has the keyboard focus, and a focused icon button shows a ring. A new rule opens with its trigger placeholder selected and the list of triggers in the panel.
- **PLT-137** · new · A small square add button follows every step and ends every inner part. It asks first for the kind of step (condition, *for each* or action), then shows a placeholder card with a muted line on what that kind does, and the list of its types in the panel. Under a placeholder the add button is pale and unavailable, so nothing follows a step until it has a type or is removed (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-138** · new · A step that holds other steps, a *for each* or an if / else block (PLT-070), is drawn as an indented arm off the main line, with rounded corners and a small tag in fixed-width capitals that names it. Its inner steps and their own add button sit in the arm, and the arm does not rejoin the main line; the main line goes on below with its own add button for the steps after it. An if / else block also has a button in its arm that adds a further part.
- **PLT-139** · new · The chain works from the keyboard as a list in reading order: each card is a button that opens its settings, and Move up and Move down move a step, so nothing depends on dragging (WCAG 2.5.7).
- **PLT-140** · new · A zoom control at the bottom centre of the canvas has zoom out, the zoom as a percentage in fixed-width figures, 100 by default, and zoom in. It stays centred when a panel narrows the canvas.
- **PLT-141** · new · Each card's second line is the step's settings as one readable sentence, updated while the step is edited, with stand-in words for fields still empty: a muted line on what a placeholder's kind does, a muted line on when a chosen trigger runs, a dark line while a step is not fully set up, a muted lead-in and a bold sentence for a comparison, and a muted italic prompt in a part whose conditions are not set. The second line is never left blank, and the sentences come from the translation files and read right to left as well (NAV-076).
- **PLT-142** · new · A step that does not fit its trigger, or still needs a choice, has a coloured outline and a warning triangle with a number on its title line, the number also in words for screen readers. The warning colour is used for nothing else on this screen.
- **PLT-143** · new · The settings of such a step start with a tinted warning: an icon, a bold heading that says the step may not fit, the advice on how to fix it (PLT-077) and a help link, and no close button (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-144** · new · With no step selected, the panel shows the rule's summary at its top, unframed and level with the top of the canvas: the icon tiles of the trigger and the actions, the name in bold, a bordered word that marks a rule made from a template, and how many steps still need input. After a gap, a framed card holds the details (PLT-079 to PLT-082), with a note on how required fields are marked first and the note on the rule's settings (PLT-081) last. The panel has no save, cancel or close of its own: the header strip saves (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-145** · new · Choosing a trigger shows a list in the panel, under a heading, with a search field that narrows it while typing and a filter button that narrows it to one group. First comes a short group of suggestions without a count (on a schedule, page published, page edited), whose rows also appear in their own groups; then the groups by where the event comes from: the rule engine itself (incoming webhook, on a schedule), Diffluence, and each app. Group headings are bold with a small count, groups and rows are in alphabetical order, and a short pointer to help ends the list.
- **PLT-146** · new · Each row in the lists of triggers, of condition types and of *for each* types is a small tinted tile with an icon and one label, with room between rows and no separators; an icon stands for a kind of event and may repeat. The condition types are, in alphabetical order: compare two values (PLT-064), if / else block (PLT-070), page condition (PLT-067), person condition (PLT-068), search query (PLT-069), and the AI condition (PLT-104) when AI is set up; the *for each* types are the sets of PLT-071 and PLT-074, also in alphabetical order.
- **PLT-147** · new · A long list in the panel keeps its heading and search field in view and scrolls only its rows, as the value picker does; a short list shrinks the panel to its content. A panel never makes the page scroll sideways.
- **PLT-148** · new · The settings of a step have a heading row with the step's icon and name and, at its end, the icon buttons of PLT-085; under it one or two sentences on what the step does, with a help link; then the step's form; and at the bottom Back and Next, each half the width, Back without a border. Back returns to the list of types, and Next finishes the step (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-149** · new · In the form of a step, labels sit above their fields in small bold text, and a required field is marked in words as well as by an asterisk. A criterion starts with a short word before its list, has its value field under it and its remove button at the end of its first row, and adding another is a quiet text button with a plus. Next stays available while a value is missing; the missing value is a message beside its field and on the card.
- **PLT-150** · later · In the settings of an if / else part, the choice between all conditions matching and at least one is two toggle buttons, each as wide as its words, the first pressed by default, above a conditions section with its add button. An empty list of conditions shows only its add button (changes the design standard, decided 2026-11-14).
- **PLT-151** · new · In an open list of choices, the current option has a tint, a bar at its start edge and accent text, so the mark does not rest on colour, and the closed field keeps showing the current choice.
- **PLT-152** · new · The value picker (PLT-075) is a floating card beside the panel of the step. Its heading has an explanation button and a close button; under it the search field and a filter button that shows the kind chosen, all kinds by default, stay in view above a line, and only the list below them scrolls. The list starts with a section of the earlier steps, one row per step that opens the values it supplies, with a way back. Escape closes the picker and returns the focus to the field.
- **PLT-160** · new · The list of *for each* types ends with the same short pointer to help as the list of triggers (PLT-145): a small heading, one sentence and one link (NAV-114), in the smallest text of the panel, scrolling with the list. Every help link on the screen of a rule opens its part of the help in the help panel beside the rule (NAV-115, NAV-116), with a way to the full article at the documentation address the administrator set (NAV-068); no link leads to a community or vendor address.
- **PLT-161** · new · Beyond PLT-066, any step whose settings need more than their one or two sentences has a collapsible explanation under its form, with an example and a link to the matching help.

## 12.10 Connections to other tools

How an instance connects to other tools, such as an issue tracker or a code host, as the roadmap's connectors describe (08); nothing is connected by itself (deliberate difference 5).

- **PLT-153** · later · An administrator adds a connection to another tool in a Connections section of the administration, through the one primary button at the end of its heading row. Under the heading, one or two sentences say what connections are for, with a help link.
- **PLT-154** · later · All connections show in one list with these columns in this order: the tool (its mark, its name, and a mark that says primary where that applies), the version the tool reports, the kind of connection, its status, and a row menu to check, change, make primary or remove it. The kinds of connection, how each signs in, and the statuses are not known yet (needs its own study).
- **PLT-155** · later · When there are several connections to the same kind of tool, one is marked primary and is used for new cards. Cards in pages refer to the connection, not to its address, so when a tool moves the administrator changes the address once and every card follows, with no separate repair.
- **PLT-156** · later · The server reaches only addresses an administrator added as a connection, through the one outward client (spec 015 D15). A token entered for a connection is a secret, stored encrypted (ADR-011) and never shown again. Linked instances (PLT-049) can show in the same list.
- **PLT-157** · later · A space administrator chooses which of the instance's connections the space uses, in a section of space settings, so that pages of the space can link to items of those tools and show them.
- **PLT-158** · later · A section of space settings lists the space's links to projects or repositories in connected tools, under a heading, a sub-heading and one sentence of explanation, and a space administrator adds and removes them there. Without links it shows a bordered box with one sentence that there are none. While no tool is connected, it says that an administrator must connect one first, and offers nothing else.
